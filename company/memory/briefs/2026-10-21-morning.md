## The one thing the research agreed on

The ideas at the top are all the same business wearing different hats: **money owed, money
available, money denied**, for owner-operated organizations that have QuickBooks and a
bookkeeper but no CFO. They share one door into the market: bookkeepers and CPAs who refer their
clients. Those people hate chasing invoices and get asked about grants constantly. That is a
company, not a list of ideas.

## My recommendation: Thesis v2, "The AI Back Office"

Start with **Get-Paid** and **Grant & Bid Studio**, both found inbound and through bookkeeper
referrals, so you are not cold-calling anyone. Use the profit to **buy a retiring bookkeeper's
practice** in year two, which the evidence says is the most reliable way to turn cash into a
seven-figure asset. Add **Denial Desk** for the medical practices in that book in year three.
Run **the Lab** alongside from day one: one day a week of agent time on experiments, prototypes
and R&D grants, with you as the one asking the questions. Full version:
`company/strategy/thesis-v2.md`.

Why I like it for you specifically: no cold calls, the tools are already wired (QuickBooks, Gmail,
Calendar, Drive, the grant-writer skill), the AI does 85-90% of the work, your role is reviewer
and builder, and the end state is something you own and can sell.

## What I did not sugarcoat

- $1M in 7 years from zero is a tail event. Only 5% of millionaires did it in under 10 years.
  These probabilities are 10-20x the population base rate, not a promise.
- The evidence that AI changes business outcomes, rather than just automating tasks, is still
  anecdotal in 2026. We are early. That is the opportunity and the risk.
- Buying a practice needs $75-150k first. The cash business exists to get there.
