# Unconventional Routes to $1M Net Worth for a Capital-Poor, Agent-Rich Operator

**Prepared:** 21 October 2026
**Profile assumed:** US-based individual, $0-10k liquid, no audience, low appetite for cold calling, some trade/industry work history, and a 24/7 AI agent team (Claude + other frontier models on a Mac mini) that can research, write, code, drive a browser and run back-office workflows.
**Goal:** fastest *credible* path to $1M net worth in under 7 years.

Method: for each play I looked for (a) primary marketplace or government data, (b) named operators with reported numbers, (c) failure-rate or default data, and (d) what the AI team can actually take over. Every figure is cited. Where a number comes from a vendor, broker, course seller or a single self-report, it is flagged **[vendor]** or **[anecdote]**; where I could not confirm a number at all it is flagged **[UNVERIFIED]**. Probabilities are my judgment calls, not measured base rates, and are stated as such.

---

## 0. The arithmetic that governs everything

Three facts shape every ranking below:

1. **$1M in 7 years from ~$0 cannot be saved out of salary-sized income; it has to be built as equity in something sellable or in debt paydown on something acquired.** Saving $1M in 84 months requires ~$12k/month of after-tax surplus. That is why cash-flow plays (fractional COO, AI installs) are ranked as *engines* that fund an equity play, not as the destination.

2. **Small businesses are priced at low multiples, and the multiple steps up with size.** BizBuySell's 2025 full-year data: median sale price $350,000, median cash flow $158,950, average cash-flow multiple 2.61x ([BizBuySell 2025 Year in Review](https://www.bizbuysell.com/blog/2025-year-in-review/)). In Q3 2025, businesses above $1M in price averaged 3.73x cash flow vs 2.30x below ([BizBuySell Insight Report](https://www.bizbuysell.com/insight-report/)). Buying at ~2.3x and selling an aggregated business at ~3.7x is the single most reliable "arbitrage" in this whole document, and it is the thesis behind every roll-up.

3. **SBA 7(a) is the only leverage available to someone with no money, and its rules changed on 1 June 2025.** SOP 50 10 8 requires a minimum 10% equity injection on complete changes of ownership; a seller note may count toward it only if it is on *full standby for the life of the loan* and covers at most half the injection (so practically 5% of project cost) ([Windsor Advantage](https://windsoradvantage.com/news/updated-sba-equity-injection-rules-what-you-need-to-know-about-sop-50-10-8); [Regalis Capital](https://learn.regaliscapital.com/guides/seller-financing-seller-notes-new-sba-rules/)). With $10k cash plus a 5% standby seller note, the maximum project size is roughly **$200k**. With $10k alone it is ~$100k. Everything bigger requires a cash engine first.

Charge-off data by loan size (SBA FOIA data, FY2010-2026, 919,729 loans, as of 31 Mar 2026): under $50k 5.9%; $50-150k 4.4%; $150-350k 4.1%; $350k-$1M 2.5%; $1-5M 1.7%; overall 4.0% ([SBA Loan Index](https://sbaloanindex.com/studies/sba-credit-benchmarks/)). Smaller loans fail more often. Median time to charge-off is ~50 months, so anything approved after 2022 looks artificially safe.

## 1. Micro-acquisitions of digital assets (SaaS, extensions, newsletters, content sites, stores)

### What the market actually pays
- **Acquire.com 2025 Multiples Report** (primary; 136 confirmed deals): median confirmed purchase price / net income multiple 3.9x in both 2024 and 2025 (4.3x in 2023); **sub-$100k businesses sold at 3.7x**, $100k-$1M at 3.9x; median 90 days on market; profitable SaaS listings averaged 71% profit margin; listings asked 10% *below* fair value got serious buyer interest 92% of the time vs 2% for asks 10% above ([PDF](https://blog.acquire.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Acquire-Multiples-Report-2025.pdf); [Jan 2026 summary](https://blog.acquire.com/acquire-com-biannual-acquisition-multiples-report-jan-2026/)). The same report benchmarks a competing marketplace's sub-$50k deals at a chaotic **0.6x-49.9x** profit spread, i.e. the very small end is mispriced in both directions. Acquire also notes public SaaS revenue multiples fell from 17x (2022) to 5.5x (2025).
- **Flippa**: content-site sales volume fell 37% in H1 2025 after Google's updates; 2025 average profit multiples cited as App 1.75x, Content 2.58x, Ecommerce 1.45x; in H1 2026 "AI Apps & Tools" transacted for the first time at an average $535,714 across 14 sales ([Flippa H1 2026](https://flippa.com/lps/insights-report-h12026); [eFinancialModels summary](https://www.efinancialmodels.com/transaction-multiples/)). Flippa's volume figures are self-reported and unaudited.
- **Empire Flippers**: content sites averaged ~24x monthly net profit in 2025 (about 2 years of profit), sold at 82% of asking; roughly 30 content sites sold by mid-2025 vs ~50 in 2024 and ~70 in 2023; listing floor is $2,000/month net ([Empire Flippers transcript](https://empireflippers.com/you-can-sell-your-business-for-life-changing-money-in-2025-heres-the-secret/); [review](https://cpiinflationcalculator.com/empire-flippers-review/)).
- **Microns.io**: sold listings from $1,000 to the tens of thousands; most listings under $50k ([Microns](https://www.microns.io/); [comparison](https://superframeworks.com/articles/best-places-sell-startup-microsaas)). Fee disclosures conflict across sources.
- **Newsletters (Duuce)**: one reported sale of a 5,100-subscriber fintech newsletter at $15,000 (asked $18,000) ≈ $3/subscriber; Duuce itself flipped for 2x in 16 months on the value of its database rather than revenue ([They Got Acquired](https://theygotacquired.com/marketplace/duuce-acquired-by-the-wisdom-group/); [Newsletter Circle](https://newslettercircle.substack.com/p/digiexamples-01-newsletter-exits)). Per-subscriber figures are my arithmetic on asking prices **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **Chrome extensions**: only broker rules of thumb exist (24-40x monthly revenue, or $0.50-$2 per weekly active user for free extensions) **[vendor]** ([ExitBid](https://exitbid.io/blog/sell-chrome-extension); [DealAlertAI](https://dealalertai.com/blog/acquire-chrome-extension-business)). No verified 2025 comps.

### Failure rates and the AI headwind
- No marketplace publishes post-close outcomes. The "92% of micro-SaaS fail in 18 months" figure circulating online has no shown methodology **[UNVERIFIED]** ([source](https://www.rockingweb.com.au/18-month-rule-micro-saas-startup-failure-analysis/)). Treat buyer-side failure rates as unknown; due-diligence guidance converges on monthly churn under 5% for sub-$5k MRR and red flags above 8% ([FE International checklist](https://www.feinternational.com/blog/saas-due-diligence-checklist-buyers)).
- **Content sites are a value trap right now.** Digital Content Next's May-June 2025 survey: median YoY Google referral decline -10% overall, -14% for non-news brands, with some members above -75%; Ahrefs (Feb 2026) measured a 58% CTR reduction for top-ranking pages under AI Overviews ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/google-ai-overviews-linked-to-25-drop-in-publisher-referral-traffic-new-data-shows/); [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/google-ai-overviews-publisher-links-search-traffic)). Google disputes the magnitude.
- **Simple SaaS is being re-rated for AI replacement risk.** M&A commentary for 2026 describes basic analytics, content generation and simple automation tools as the categories facing the steepest multiple pressure; durability comes from workflow depth, proprietary data and switching costs ([SaaSRise](https://www.saasrise.com/blog/the-saas-m-a-report-2026-2); [L40](https://www.l40.com/insights/ai-impact-saas-valuation)).

### Financing
SBA 7(a) can finance online businesses; lenders verify revenue through Stripe/Shopify/PayPal exports tied to tax returns, require an independent valuation above $250k, and the obstacle for SaaS "is almost never eligibility, it is collateral," which is solved with a larger seller note or a personal real-estate lien ([Ecommerce Lending](https://www.ecommercelending.com/sba-loan/saas); [GoSBA](https://gosbaloans.com/blog/sba-loan-online-business/)). Realistically, SBA is irrelevant below ~$150k; sub-$50k deals are cash or seller-financed.

### What a $10k buyer can actually do
At 3.7x net income, $10k buys ~$225/month of profit. That is a learning asset, not a wealth asset. The credible version of this play is a **portfolio flywheel**: buy 2-3 neglected $5-20k assets with obvious, AI-fixable defects (no onboarding emails, broken Stripe dunning, no SEO, unanswered support), lift profit 50-150% in 6 months with agent labor, hold for cash or resell at 3.5-4x. The operator-reported example of a scheduling tool bought for $3,800 and grown to $1,200 MRR is illustrative, not documented **[anecdote]** ([BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/buy-micro-saas-grow-acquisition-playbook-2026)).

**Scorecard**
| Who pays | Users of the acquired product (existing MRR) |
|---|---|
| Capital needed | $5-25k to start; scales with recycled cash |
| Time to $10k/mo | 24-48 months via 4-8 assets, if ~half of acquisitions work |
| Path to $1M <7y | Portfolio throwing off $250k/yr profit sells at ~3.9x = $1M. Requires ~10x compounding of the starting stake. |
| Probability (my estimate) | **~8-12%** |
| AI-operable share | **80-90%** (support, dev, SEO, email, dunning, diligence screening) |
| Cold-selling burden | Low (buying, not selling; outreach to sellers is warm email) |
| Regulatory risk | Low (privacy/ToS, Stripe compliance) |
| Evidence quality | **4/5** for pricing, **1/5** for post-close outcomes |

## 2. Boring offline cash-flow business via SBA 7(a), AI back office

### Prices and cash flow by niche (closed-sale data where available)
- **All small business (BizBuySell 2025):** median price $350k, median cash flow $158,950, 2.61x cash flow; service businesses $340k median and 2.52x ([BizBuySell](https://www.bizbuysell.com/blog/2025-year-in-review/); [Q4 2025 report](https://www.bizbuysell.com/news/bizbuysell-2025-fourth-quarter-insight-report/)).
- **Laundromats:** BizBuySell sold data 2021-2025 shows 3.65x average SDE (3.50x median; 2025 average 4.12x), median price $250k, median SDE $76.6k; Coin Laundry Association guidance 3-5x net ([BizBuySell laundromat benchmarks](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/valuation-benchmarks/laundromats-coin-laundry/)). Once you pay a manager and reserve for machine replacement, one analysis cuts the median store's owner earnings from ~$77k to ~$49k of hands-off cash flow, making the 4.12x price closer to **6.5x** what a passive owner keeps **[single analysis]** ([Business Model Analyst](https://businessmodelanalyst.com/laundromat-passive-premium-owner-earnings/)). SBA FOIA data on 8,042 laundromat loans (1991-June 2026): 2005-2007 cohort write-off rate **30.5%**; 2010s cohort 4.6% (immature); median loan size rose from $125k (2005-07) to $441,500 (2021-23) while resale owner earnings fell from $90k to $77k; median time to failure 5 years ([Wash Weekly](https://www.washweekly.com/what-8-042-federal-loans-say-about-buying-a-laundromat/)). Laundromats are *not* the AI play: the cost is machines, utilities and rent, none of which an agent reduces.
- **Pest control:** BizBuySell 2025: 28 closed sales at **2.35x SDE**, median $237,500; under-$200k-revenue routes ~1.7x ([BizBuySell pest control](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/valuation-benchmarks/pest-control/)). Broker claims of 3-6x are asking-price talk **[vendor]**.
- **Pool routes:** priced at 6-12x *monthly* recurring billing (premium Sun Belt routes 12-14x), i.e. roughly 0.5-1.0x annual revenue; 2-3.5x SDE for owner-operator routes **[vendor]** ([Clearly Acquired](https://www.clearlyacquired.com/blog/pool-business-acquisition-multiples-valuation-guide); [Pool Founder](https://www.poolfounder.com/pool-route-valuation)).
- **Insurance agencies:** BizBuySell 2025: small owner-operated agencies **2.68x SDE, 1.53x revenue, median $650k**; books alone 1.0-1.5x commissions ([Renegade Insurance](https://renegadeinsurance.com/insurance-agency-valuation/); [Sofer Advisors](https://soferadvisors.com/insights/blog/how-to-value-an-insurance-agency-multiples-and-methods/)). Requires state licensing and carrier appointments.
- **Accounting / bookkeeping / tax practices:** BizBuySell 2025 benchmark **1.11x revenue, 2.34x earnings**; sole-practitioner tax-only firms with retiring owners often 0.5-0.8x revenue; seller notes of 30-50% of price and 1-3 year client-retention earn-outs are standard; non-CPAs are now actively buying CPA firms ([CT Acquisitions](https://ctacquisitions.com/how-to-sell-accounting-firm/); [Acquisition Ace](https://www.acquisitionace.io/learn/industries/accounting-firm); [CPA Practice Advisor, July 2025](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2025/07/15/the-rise-of-non-cpas-in-cpa-firm-acquisitions/164966/)). This is the one offline niche where **the seller finances most of the deal as a matter of custom**, which is exactly what a $10k buyer needs.
- **Vending routes:** realistic $100-300 net/machine/month; 20% of machines produce 80% of profit **[vendor]** ([Nav](https://www.nav.com/blog/vending-machine-business-1864566/); [NeuroShop](https://neuroshop.tech/blog/understanding-realistic-vending-machine-profits)). Capital-heavy per dollar of profit; AI adds almost nothing.

### Default and outcome data
- Overall 7(a) charge-off 4.0% (FY2010-26 cohort); $350k-$1M loans 2.5%; Professional/Technical Services 3.7%; Administrative & Support (landscaping, cleaning) 3.9%; Finance & Insurance 2.5% ([SBA Loan Index](https://sbaloanindex.com/studies/sba-credit-benchmarks/)). The FY2015-2019 cohort is at 6.92% by count ([Crestmont](https://www.crestmontcapital.com/blog/sba-loan-default-rate-statistics)).
- **Self-funded searchers (SIG 2023 study, 279 respondents, 109 acquisitions):** 58% used SBA 7(a), 45% a seller note; 53% closed within 12 months; successful acquirers submitted 6.9 LOIs on average; 83% of deals closed below 5x EBITDA; 30% of targets had under $500k EBITDA; reported capital-loss rate 5% (self-selected, mostly unrealized marks) ([investing.io summary](https://investing.io/self-funded-search-fund-statistics/)).
- **Counterweight (Yale SOM, Oct 2025):** across 1,192 investor observations from 12 search-fund investors, 64% of searches converted to an actionable deal, **58% of deal MOIC outcomes were ≤1.99x**, only 2% exceeded 10x, it is "a fifty-fifty chance whether a search results in a 1.0x MOIC or higher," and *no* investor in the sample matched Stanford's headline 4.5x MOIC / 35.1% IRR ([Yale case PDF](https://som.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2025-10/How%20are%20Search%20Fund%20Investors%20Really%20Faring.pdf); [Stanford 2024](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6455268783d6938b9451ea80/669fbcb3e5f07cc9a6093751_StanfordGSB_Study_2024.pdf)). The Stanford numbers are for MBA-backed funds buying $1-10M EBITDA companies; they are not a base rate for a $200k deal.

### What AI actually removes (and does not)
Removes or shrinks: bookkeeping and reconciliation (vendor-cited 4.8 hours saved per client per month, Sage 2025 report via secondary source; 20 hours/client reduced to 2-3 hours of review per one vendor guide **[vendor]**) ([StealthAgents](https://stealthagents.com/research/ai-bookkeeping-automation-statistics-2026); [AI Scale Labs](https://aiscalelabs.com/ai-for-accounting-firms/)); AR chasing; scheduling/dispatch; quoting; review responses; ad management; inbound phone via voice agents (wholesale ~$0.06/min, resold at $0.15-0.25/min **[vendor]** ([Leadlock](https://www.leadlock.ai/blog/white-label-ai-voice-agents-gohighlevel-charge-clients/))). Does not remove: trucks, technicians, licenses, machines, rent, the personal guarantee, or the seller's relationships with clients.

### Down-payment math (worked)
$400k service business at 2.6x on $155k SDE: 10% injection = $40k (max $20k from standby seller note). SBA $360k over 10 years at ~10.5% ≈ $58k/yr debt service. Pre-tax, pre-owner-salary cash ≈ $97k. Principal paydown ~$25-35k/yr early. If AI lifts SDE to ~$200k by year 3 and you sell at 2.6x in year 6, equity ≈ $520k price - ~$180k remaining debt + retained cash ≈ **$450-600k**. $1M needs either a second acquisition (see Play 3) or the size step-up to 3.7x.

**Scorecard**
| Who pays | Existing customers of the acquired business |
|---|---|
| Capital needed | $20-60k cash (so: not month one; needs a cash engine or a seller-financed practice) |
| Time to $10k/mo | Day 1 after close (SDE already exists), 9-18 months to close |
| Path to $1M <7y | Debt paydown + AI margin lift + one add-on, or sale above $1M at the higher multiple |
| Probability | **~15-20%** conditional on raising the injection; ~10% unconditional |
| AI-operable share | 40-60% of owner hours (back office), 0% of field labor |
| Cold-selling burden | Low-moderate (deal sourcing is email/broker outreach; no customer cold calls) |
| Regulatory risk | Moderate (licensing in HVAC/pest/insurance; PG on the loan is the real risk) |
| Evidence quality | **5/5** pricing and default data; 3/5 for AI savings claims |

## 3. Tiny roll-up: 3-5 micro-businesses in one niche

Evidence of the mechanism is strong; evidence of individuals doing it at $200k scale is thin.
- The multiple step-up (2.30x below $1M vs 3.73x above, BizBuySell Q3 2025) is the entire thesis ([BizBuySell](https://www.bizbuysell.com/insight-report/)).
- PE bought nearly 800 HVAC/plumbing/electrical companies 2022-Oct 2024 (PitchBook via WSJ, cited at [Profitability Partners](https://profitabilitypartners.io/home-services-private-equity-acquirers/)); independent sponsors roughly doubled to ~1,400 firms by June 2026 (McGuireWoods estimate via [Peony](https://www.peony.ink/blog/independent-sponsor-business-services-capital-partners)). That is your eventual *buyer*, and also your competition for anything above ~$1M EBITDA. Below $300k SDE, PE is absent.
- Apex Service Partners started with two Florida businesses ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.net/blog/roll-up-strategy)). No documented case of a solo buyer rolling up 3-5 sub-$500k businesses in 2025 surfaced **[gap in evidence]**.
- Constraint: SBA 7(a) caps at $5M aggregate per borrower, and each acquisition after the first can often be structured as expansion financing with lower injection; verify with lender ([Live Oak](https://www.liveoak.bank/blog/financing-your-business-acquisition-with-sba-7a-loan)).

Where consolidation savings are real for a tiny operator: one AI back office (books, AR, phones, scheduling, marketing) across 3-5 practices or routes. Bookkeeping/tax practices are the best candidate because the "ops" *are* the back office. Home-service routes second (pool/pest/cleaning: route density matters).

**Scorecard:** capital $50-150k cumulative (mostly recycled cash flow + SBA); time to $10k/mo = same as Play 2; path to $1M = cross $1M revenue / $300k+ SDE and sell at 3.5x+; probability **~10%** (it is Play 2 executed three times without a stumble); AI-operable 50-70%; cold selling low-moderate; regulatory moderate; evidence **4/5** on mechanism, **1/5** on micro-scale practitioners.

## 4. IP and licensing (Stephen Key model, music/stock libraries, patents)

- Stephen Key's own figures: standard royalty ~5% of net sales, range ~3% (high volume) to 7%; a provisional patent filing costs ~$70-$150; income examples are his anecdotes, not a survey ([inventRight](https://inventright.com/innovate-this/get-a-higher-royalty-rate/); [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/stephen-key/how-to-get-a-higher-royalty-rate-for-your-invention.html)). A 5% royalty on a product wholesaling $1M/yr is $50k/yr; you would need several hits and an improbable hit rate. No independent income data exists **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **Stock images:** self-reports of $10 after 45 days and 25 downloads at day 70; a $3,976 first-year claim on a promotional page **[anecdote/vendor]** ([Medium](https://medium.com/@pranithnaikpranit/earnings-after-45-days-of-uploading-ai-images-to-adobe-stock-started-jan-1-2025-79ca2f6a00f0); [AutoKeyWorder](https://autokeyworder.com/blog/ai-stock-photo-earnings-3976/)).
- **Music:** fully AI-generated works are ineligible for US copyright and PRO registration; ASCAP/BMI/SOCAN accept works only where a human plays a meaningful role; Deezer reported up to 85% of streams on AI music were fraudulent in 2025 ([Royalty Solutions](https://www.royaltysolutionscorp.com/blog/ai-in-music-new-policy); [Dynamoi](https://dynamoi.com/learn/ai-music-distribution/spotify-ai-music-policy)). Library buyouts run $200-$8,000 per track **[vendor]**. The asset you would be building is legally unprotectable if it is AI-made.

**Scorecard:** capital <$2k; time to $10k/mo: unlikely; path to $1M: requires multiple licensed hits; probability **<3%**; AI-operable 70% (design, sell-sheets, outreach) but the outreach *is* cold pitching to manufacturers; regulatory low (copyright ineligibility is the issue); evidence **2/5**.

## 5. Selling systems, not services: one-time "AI operating system" installs for a trade; white-label agents

- Price points actually quoted: SMB setup $1,500-$5,000 one-time; $2,500-$7,500 + $300-$800/mo starter packages; $3,000-$10,000 setups for mid-market; retainers $500-$5,000/mo **[vendor]** ([Taskip](https://taskip.net/ai-automation-agency-pricing/); [SuperDupr](https://superdupr.com/blog/ai-automation-pricing)). One home-services chatbot example at $5,000 setup + $400/mo **[anecdote]** ([Cursive](https://cursivemedia.com/blog/ai-automation-agency-cost)).
- White-label stack: GoHighLevel SaaS mode requires the $497/mo Agency Pro plan; testimonials of 28 clients/$14k MRR and 17 clients/$13.5k MRR at 68% gross margin come from platform vendors ([Autocalls](https://autocalls.ai/white-label); [Leadlock](https://www.leadlock.ai/blog/white-label-ai-voice-agents-how-agencies-scale-to-100-clients/)) **[vendor]**. No independent survey of what AI agencies actually earn exists **[gap]**.
- The trade-specific demand signal is real: home-service contractors pay $45-$120+ per shared lead on Angi and $51-$162 per Local Services Ads lead; LocaliQ's 3,211-campaign study put blended Google Ads cost per lead at $90.92 ([HomeShowOff](https://homeshowoff.com/real-cost-of-contractor-leads/); [SmartTools](https://smartoolsforbiz.com/blog/average-cost-per-lead-home-services); [PipelineOn](https://pipelineon.com/blog/home-service-marketing-benchmarks-2026/)). A speed-to-lead voice agent that converts 20% more of $90 leads pays for a $5k install quickly; that is the pitch.

The problem is not the product, it is distribution: this is a sales business. Without an audience or warm network it requires exactly the cold outreach the Owner dislikes. It becomes attractive only when fused with Play 9 (sell into the Owner's own former industry).

**Scorecard:** capital <$3k; time to $10k/mo 6-12 months *if* 10-15 warm prospects exist; path to $1M: a $300-500k/yr agency sells at ~2-3x SDE at best (agencies trade below SaaS), so $1M requires converting installs into a product (Play 9); probability **~8%** standalone, **~15%** fused with Play 9; AI-operable 70% (build, onboarding, support) / 20% (sales); cold-selling **high**; regulatory low-moderate (TCPA for outbound AI calls, state AI-disclosure laws); evidence **2/5**.

## 6. Government and institutional money

- **SBIR/STTR:** authority lapsed 30 Sep 2025 and was reauthorized 13 Apr 2026 through 30 Sep 2031 (S. 3971); FY2025 awards fell to ~4,729 vs a 6,713 five-year average, Phase I down 32%; unspent FY2026 funds roll to FY2027, implying a catch-up wave of solicitations ([Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/sbirsttr-programs-reauthorized-after-six-month-lapse); [Federal News Network](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/commentary/2026/04/the-sbir-restart-wont-be-easy/)). Phase I ceiling ~$314k (2025), reported ~$336k now **[secondary]**; NSF Phase I success ~14-20%, first-timers ~13-15% **[secondary]** ([Cada](https://www.getcada.com/insights/nsf-sbir-success-rates); [GrantSights](https://grantsights.com/blog/sbir-phase-1-grant-guide)). The AI team's edge is proposal drafting at scale, but the new law tightened "SBIR mill" rules, and a Phase I needs a real R&D hypothesis and a principal investigator primarily employed by the firm. It funds a company; it is not net worth.
- **8(a):** requires social/economic disadvantage, personal net worth under the threshold, ~90-120 day certification **[vendor]**; sole-source ceiling $5.5M for services from 1 Oct 2025; the USDS 8(a) Digital Service Initiative exists for software ([SLED AI](https://www.sledai.com/blog/8a-certification-guide/); [LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/8a-sole-source-contracts-rules-limits-and-process/); [TechFAR Hub](https://techfarhub.usds.gov/get-started/8a/)). No data on new-entrant time to first award **[gap]**. Only relevant if the Owner is eligible, and government contracting is relationship-selling.
- **Medicaid waiver home care:** median net margin ~9.7%; Medicaid/waiver payer mix yields single-digit margins; startup $40-80k; no reliable enrollment timelines ([Boss Healthcare](https://www.boss.healthcare/post/home-care-margins); [Clarify Capital](https://clarifycapital.com/blog/home-care-agency-owner-income-2025)). Labor-intensive, low-margin, heavily regulated: wrong fit.
- **Fiscal sponsorship:** sponsors charge 5-15% of funds; it is a vehicle for grants to nonprofits, not a route to personal net worth ([HCB](https://help.hcb.hackclub.com/article/18-what-is-the-fiscal-sponsorship-fee-and-how-much-is-it-why-does-hcb-take-a-fiscal-sponsorship-fee-how-does-it-compare-to-other-fiscal-sponsors); [OpenGrants](https://opengrants.io/what-is-a-fiscal-sponsor-a-simple-guide-for-for-profit-ventures/)).

**Scorecard (SBIR line):** capital <$2k; time to $10k/mo: a Phase I award pays ~$275-335k over 6-12 months, so "income" is lumpy; path to $1M: only if Phase II ($1-2M) leads to a sellable company; probability **~5%**; AI-operable 60% (writing, compliance), 0% of the science; cold-selling low (written proposals) but program-officer relationships matter; regulatory high (compliance, audits); evidence **4/5** on program facts, **2/5** on odds for a solo first-timer.

## 7. Community, marketplace, job board, local newsletter, directory

- **Local newsletters (best-documented sub-play):** Naptown Scoop (Annapolis) ~$200k revenue on ~18k subscribers in 2023, later reported at 21k subscribers and ~$320k/yr; implied $11-15 per subscriber per year, mostly local ad sales; affiliate income trivial ([Creator Spotlight](https://www.creatorspotlight.com/p/ryan-sneddon-naptown-scoop); [Breaking & Building Leaders](https://www.breakingandbuildingleaders.com/p/how-to-build-a-killer-local-newsletter-making-320k-even-in-a-small-town)) **[self-reported]**. 6AM City (450k subscribers) cut 30% of staff, shrank from 30 cities to 19, expects first profit in 2026, and is "seeding new markets with AI-powered newsletters that require minimal human involvement" ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/media/6am-city-layoffs-artificial-intelligence/); [Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/news/6am-city-ryan-heafy-interview/)). Founder claims $60k of marketing to reach 50k subscribers and 70% margin per city at 36 months **[founder claim]**.
- **The AI-native comp:** Good Daily, a one-person operation of Matthew Henderson, ran ~350 AI-produced local newsletters with ~600k subscribers (~1,700 per market), was questioned by Nieman Lab over subscriber integrity, had no evidence of meaningful revenue, and was acquired by 6AM City in 2025 for an undisclosed sum, largely for the founder's automation skills (he became VP Engineering) ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2025/01/inside-a-network-of-ai-generated-newsletters-targeting-small-town-america/); [Life of Scoop](https://www.lifeofscoop.co/p/why-6am-city-bought-good-daily)). Lesson: AI can manufacture the product and the audience cheaply; the money is in *local ad sales*, which is a human relationship business.
- **Directories:** OpenAlternative ~$6,500/mo on 70k visits after ~1 year; SaaSHub ~$10k MRR on 1M+ views at $99 featured listings; an anonymous directory exit at ~$5k/mo priced under $50k **[vendor]**; across 11 directories tracked for 24 months, organic traffic is broadly declining ([DirStarter](https://dirstarter.com/blog/directory-website-examples); [DirectoryGems](https://www.directorygems.com/case-study/how-directory-sites-survive-ai-era)).
- **Paid communities:** on Skool, ~70% of communities are free; among paid ones the modeled median is ~$1,200/mo; >$10k/mo is a small minority; communities past 1,000 members median >$40k/mo **[modeled, upper bound]** ([profitable.app](https://profitable.app/skool/stats)). Skool's verified "$1M+ lifetime" list includes trade communities such as Wealthy Plumber and Six Figure Barber Mentorship ([SkoolPrep](https://skoolprep.com/skool-millionaire-communities)); the "Blue-Collar Biz Accelerator at $1M MRR" claim circulating online could not be verified **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **Job boards:** MoAIJobs ~$2.3k/mo; Remote Rocketship ~$6.5k MRR; Remote OK figures conflict wildly across sources ([Gaps](https://gaps.com/job-boards/)).
- **Rank-and-rent lead sites:** all income claims come from course sellers (Ippei: "$3,000/mo per site," 55 paying renters) **[vendor]** ([Ippei](https://ippei.com/rank-and-rent-websites/)). The underlying lead values ($45-$160) are real; the SEO durability is not.

**Scorecard (local newsletter line):** capital $2-10k (ads to seed subscribers); time to $10k/mo 18-30 months per market; path to $1M: 3-5 adjacent markets at $250k each sells at perhaps 2-3x revenue **[UNVERIFIED; no newsletter-network comps found]**; probability **~8%**; AI-operable 80% of production, 20% of ad sales; cold-selling **moderate-high** (local advertisers); regulatory low (CAN-SPAM); evidence **3/5**.

## 8. Attention arbitrage: AI long-form education in underserved languages / faceless channels

- Spanish-language YouTube RPMs are brutal: a translated geopolitics channel reported ~$8.6k on 3.3M views in 30 days (≈$2.61 RPM) **[self-report via vendor]** ([Satura](https://saturaai.com/blog/how-to-build-spanish-faceless-youtube-channel-by-translating-proven-videos-8-6k-mo-icgf4h)); GENIAL (32M subs) is estimated at only $4-18k/mo because of Latin American ad geography **[estimate]** ([GoFaceless](https://www.gofaceless.ai/en/blog/faceless-youtube-channel-examples)). A small English educational channel reported $895 total from 12 videos over two years ([Unkoa](https://www.unkoa.com/faceless-youtube-10000-month-2025/)).
- The audience is real and growing: Hispanic-owned employer firms reached ~496,000 in 2023 (+49.5% in five years) plus 5.3M nonemployer firms; Latino-owned firms grew 7.7%/yr vs 0.46% for all employer firms; they are 1.5x more likely to need additional financing ([Census](https://census.gov/library/stories/2024/10/hispanic-owned-businesses.html); [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/charting-the-surge-in-latino-or-hispanic-owned-businesses-in-the-us/); [UCLA LPPI](https://latino.ucla.edu/press/at-latino-owned-businesses-across-the-u-s-drove-post-pandemic-growth-despite-systemic-barriers/)). No data on Spanish-language business-education demand surfaced **[gap]**.

Ad revenue cannot carry this; it only works as top-of-funnel for a paid product (bookkeeping, tax, licensing prep, community) sold to US Hispanic contractors, which is Play 7 with a language edge and faces the same selling problem.

**Scorecard:** capital <$2k; time to $10k/mo 24+ months and only with a paid product; path to $1M: weak; probability **~3-5%**; AI-operable 90% of production; cold-selling low (content) but monetization needs sales; regulatory low; evidence **2/5**.

## 9. Vertical AI tool sold into the Owner's own former industry

This is the one play where "no audience" is false: the Owner has a network of ex-employers, ex-colleagues and vendors who already trust them.
- Pattern evidence: Vantaca was built as internal tooling inside the founder's own HOA-management company before becoming a product; ServiceTitan's founder built dispatch software for the trades after living the chaos; a dentist-turned-PM bootstrapped Auxpanel to profitability in year two with zero marketing **[Medium post]** ([Practical Founders](https://practicalfounders.com/articles/); [ServiceTitan history](https://cloud.substack.com/p/from-30m-to-11b-the-servicetitan); [Medium](https://medium.com/@urano10/vertical-saas-micro-niches-beyond-the-obvious-01e0adf16a98)). Investors note PE-backed vertical founders are "consistently former operators within the markets they're now serving." First-customer playbooks uniformly say start with former colleagues ([SaaS Club](https://saasclub.io/podcast/first-customers/)).
- Exit math: Acquire.com's 3.9x profit median applies to small SaaS; businesses with low churn, growth and net revenue retention are the "10x+ bucket" ([Acquire PDF](https://blog.acquire.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Acquire-Multiples-Report-2025.pdf)). A vertical tool at $25k MRR with 70% margins is ~$800k-$1M at 3.9x profit, more on a revenue basis to a strategic.
- The AI-replacement discount does not bite as hard here: the durable categories are workflow depth, proprietary data and compliance ([SaaSRise](https://www.saasrise.com/blog/the-saas-m-a-report-2026-2)). Industry-specific compliance, forms and integrations are exactly what an insider knows and a generic agent does not.

**Scorecard:** capital <$5k; time to $10k/mo 9-18 months (10-20 customers at $500-1,000/mo, sold warm); path to $1M: reach ~$250-300k ARR at 70%+ margin and sell at 3.9-5x profit, or keep compounding; probability **~12-15%**; AI-operable 85% (build, support, docs, onboarding) with the Owner doing 10-20 warm conversations; cold-selling **low-moderate** (warm network, then referrals); regulatory depends on vertical (healthcare/finance higher); evidence **3/5** (consistent patterns, few audited small-scale numbers).

## 12. Defending the top three

### #1 Seller-financed accounting/bookkeeping/tax practice, then a micro roll-up

**Why it is the best fit.** It is the only offline niche where (a) the back office *is* the business, so the agent team's leverage is maximal; (b) sellers customarily carry 30-50% of the price and tie it to retention, which both reduces cash needed and aligns the seller to help; (c) BizBuySell shows the category at 1.11x revenue / 2.34x earnings, above its five-year average, which signals buyer demand for your eventual exit; (d) a demographic wave of retiring sole practitioners is creating supply, and non-CPAs are now closing these deals ([CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2025/07/15/the-rise-of-non-cpas-in-cpa-firm-acquisitions/164966/)); (e) SBA charge-offs in Professional & Technical Services are 3.7% and lower still above $350k ([SBA Loan Index](https://sbaloanindex.com/studies/sba-credit-benchmarks/)).

**Worked numbers (illustrative).** Target: $180k revenue bookkeeping + tax practice, owner retiring, 40% SDE margin ($72k). Price at 0.9x = $162k. Structure: $16k injection (10k cash + 6k standby seller note), $40k additional seller note (amortizing after year 2), $106k SBA 7(a) over 10 years at ~10.5% ≈ $17k/yr. Year-1 cash after debt and a $20k transition fee to the seller ≈ $35k; agent-run close, reconciliation and tax-prep assembly lift margin to ~55% by year 2 (consistent with the 4.8 hrs/client/month vendor claims, which you should treat as a ceiling). Repeat in years 2, 3 and 4 at similar sizes. Year 6: ~$700-800k revenue, ~$350k SDE, debt ~$200k. At 2.34x SDE / 1.1x revenue that is ~$800-900k enterprise value; equity plus retained cash plausibly clears **$1M by year 6-7** *if* client retention through four transitions averages 85%+ (**retention rates are not published; this is the key unverified assumption**).

**What kills it.** Client attrition at handover; seasonality (tax revenue lands Jan-Apr while debt is monthly); licensing (a PTIN is trivial; an EA credential takes months and is needed to represent clients; bookkeeping needs none); the personal guarantee; and lender insistence on "relevant experience," which the Owner mitigates by keeping the seller on contract and by holding an EA or hiring one part-time.

**First 90 days.**
- *Days 1-15:* Agents build a deal-sourcing pipeline: scrape BizBuySell, Accounting Practice Sales, APS, Poe Group, state CPA society classifieds, and Acquire; filter to $120-350k revenue, owner 60+, 70%+ recurring bookkeeping/payroll, no audit work. Draft a one-page buyer profile and a warm, non-salesy outreach email (this is email, not calls). Register for the EA exam; schedule Part 1 within 60 days. Get pre-qualified with two SBA lenders that lend to practices (Live Oak and a local preferred lender).
- *Days 16-45:* 15-25 seller conversations by email and video. Agents produce a standard diligence pack per target: client list ageing, revenue by client, churn last 3 years, fee per client vs market, software stack, hours per client. Submit 3-5 LOIs (SIG data says ~7 LOIs per closed deal).
- *Days 46-75:* Pick one; negotiate 30-50% seller note with 24-month retention adjustment; SBA application with independent valuation if >$250k. Agents build the operating system in parallel: QuickBooks/Xero agent workflows for categorization and reconciliation, AR chasing, client portal, document intake, monthly close checklist, tax organizer automation.
- *Days 76-90:* Close or move to the next LOI. Joint letter to clients from seller and buyer; 100% of clients receive a personal call from the *seller* (not you). Set 12-month KPIs: retention ≥90%, owner hours per client cut 50%, SDE margin +10 points.

### #2 Vertical AI product into the Owner's own industry (install first, SaaS second)

**Why.** It turns the Owner's "no audience" into a false constraint: ex-employers and peers are a warm list of 20-50 people who already believe the Owner understands their work. Vertical tools with real workflow depth are the category *least* exposed to the 2026 AI-replacement re-rating. The agent team can build, document, support and iterate 24/7, so the Owner's scarce hours go entirely into the 10-20 conversations that matter. Selling first as a $5-15k install plus $300-1,500/mo retainer (the price points the market already quotes) funds the product and produces the case studies; converting the retainers into subscription seats produces the asset. At Acquire's 3.9x profit median a $300k ARR, 70% margin tool is ~$800k; to a strategic acquirer in the vertical it is more.

**What kills it.** Picking a problem the Owner remembers but the industry has since solved; building a feature the incumbent vertical platform (ServiceTitan, Jobber, Procore, etc.) ships next quarter; per-seat pricing in a vertical where AI shrinks headcount; and compliance exposure in regulated verticals.

**First 90 days.**
- *Days 1-10:* Owner writes down the 10 most painful, repetitive, document-heavy workflows from their last three jobs. Agents research each: incumbent software, forums, job postings (what are companies hiring humans to do?), regulatory forms. Pick the one with a clear owner, a clear dollar cost, and no good incumbent.
- *Days 11-30:* 12-15 warm conversations (video/coffee, not cold). Agents prepare per-person briefs and follow-ups. Goal: three paid pilots at $2.5-5k install + $500/mo, scoped to one workflow, delivered in 30 days.
- *Days 31-75:* Agents build and ship pilots on a boring, maintainable stack; Owner reviews daily. Instrument everything: hours saved, errors avoided, dollars recovered. Weekly check-ins with pilot customers, run by the Owner, prepped by agents.
- *Days 76-90:* Convert pilots to annual retainers; write three one-page case studies; ask each pilot customer for two introductions (referral, not cold). Decide on productization: if two of three pilots renew and refer, package as self-serve SaaS with the install as onboarding. Target: $10k MRR by month 9-12.

### #3 Micro-acquisition flywheel

**Why.** It is the purest expression of the Owner's edge: 24/7 agent labor applied to assets whose sellers are tired. The market data is primary and consistent (3.7x net income under $100k on Acquire; 90-day median time on market; chaotic pricing under $50k on other platforms means mispricings exist). Capital needed matches what the Owner has. No selling, no personal guarantee, no licenses.

**What kills it.** Buying content/SEO assets (traffic is structurally declining under AI Overviews); buying thin wrappers around a model API that a competitor ships free; undisclosed churn; and over-paying for revenue that depends on the founder's personal audience. The honest ceiling: it takes ~10x compounding of a $10-25k stake to reach $1M; most operators will stall at a few thousand dollars a month of side income. It is ranked #3 because it is the best *training ground* and parallel cash contributor, not because it is the likeliest path alone.

**First 90 days.**
- *Days 1-14:* Agents build a screener across Acquire, Microns, Flippa, Empire Flippers and Duuce: profitable, 12+ months revenue history, <5% monthly churn, no SEO dependence, revenue verifiable via Stripe/processor exports, asking ≤3.5x trailing net profit, seller stated reason "no time." Set a hard rule: no content sites, no AI-wrapper SaaS.
- *Days 15-45:* Request processor exports and churn cohorts on 10-15 listings; agents produce a standard diligence memo and a 90-day uplift plan for each (what is broken that agents can fix: onboarding, dunning, support backlog, pricing page, integrations). Offer on three at 10-20% below asking (Acquire data: asks 10% under fair value get 92% serious interest; the mirror image is that lowball offers on stale listings get accepted).
- *Days 46-90:* Close one for $5-15k via escrow (Acquire's or Escrow.com). Agents execute the uplift plan; weekly metrics review. Success threshold at day 90 post-close: +30% MRR or +10 points margin. If hit, reinvest cash into a second asset; if not, list it back at 3.5x and move on.

## 13. Things the Owner should not do, with the evidence

- Do not buy a laundromat for the "passive AI business" story: machines, rent and utilities are the cost, owner earnings have fallen while prices rose, and the 2005-07 SBA cohort lost 30% ([Wash Weekly](https://www.washweekly.com/what-8-042-federal-loans-say-about-buying-a-laundromat/)).
- Do not buy content sites or build rank-and-rent SEO farms: publisher referrals are down 10-25% with long tails to -75%, and content-site transaction volume has collapsed year over year ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/google-ai-overviews-linked-to-25-drop-in-publisher-referral-traffic-new-data-shows/); [Empire Flippers](https://empireflippers.com/you-can-sell-your-business-for-life-changing-money-in-2025-heres-the-secret/)).
- Do not build AI music or stock libraries as "assets": fully AI-generated works cannot be copyrighted or PRO-registered ([Royalty Solutions](https://www.royaltysolutionscorp.com/blog/ai-in-music-new-policy)).
- Do not treat Stanford's 35% IRR as a base rate: Yale's investor-level data shows 58% of deal outcomes at or below 1.99x and no investor matching the index ([Yale SOM](https://som.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2025-10/How%20are%20Search%20Fund%20Investors%20Really%20Faring.pdf)).
- Do not plan on seller notes replacing the SBA injection: post-June 2025 they count only on full standby for the life of the loan and only up to half the injection ([Regalis](https://learn.regaliscapital.com/guides/seller-financing-seller-notes-new-sba-rules/)).

1. Client retention rates after small accounting-practice transfers (ask brokers at APS/Poe Group for their data; none is published).
2. Post-close outcomes for sub-$100k marketplace acquisitions (Acquire.com has the data; ask them).
3. Independent revenue data for AI automation agencies and fractional COOs (all current figures are self-published).

5. Whether the Owner is 8(a)-eligible and whether their industry has an SBIR-shaped R&D problem; both change the ranking of Play 6.
