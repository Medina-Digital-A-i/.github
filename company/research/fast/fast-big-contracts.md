# Fast, Big Contracts: Documented Paths to $1M+ in 12 Months for a Brand-New, Cash-Poor US Small Business with an AI Agent Team

Prepared 2026-10-21. Research method: ~45 web searches, primary-source fetches (CRS, SBA, GAO, CSIS, GMU Baroni Center, eCFR/FAR, NYC Comptroller, Tradewinds), and an original pull from the USAspending.gov API (FY2025 awards). Vendor/consultant/course-seller claims are flagged **[VENDOR CLAIM]**. Where a figure is my own calculation it is marked **[CALC]**.

---

## 0. Bottom line up front

1. **The documented base rate is brutal.** Roughly 10,000–12,000 entities win their first federal prime contract each year (GovSpend, FY21–FY25) against a prime-contractor pool of ~120,000 and a SAM registrant pool several times larger. Among FY2025 set-aside awards of $1M+ in 20 services NAICS codes, only ~6–7% went to entities with no prior federal contract history, and most of those were joint ventures, Alaska Native/tribal subsidiaries, SBIR Phase II awardees, or spin-outs of established firms **[CALC, USAspending API, see §1.4]**. A genuinely new, independent firm winning a $1M+ federal prime in year one is a low-single-digit-percent event.
2. **The only well-documented year-one $1M+ pattern is "borrowed past performance":** joint ventures and subcontracts where an established partner's record carries the bid (13 CFR 125.8(e) forces agencies to credit each JV member's work). The PrimeRFP "America at 250" list of first-time federal primes with $2.3M–$635M first awards is dominated by JVs and affiliates of existing contractors.
3. **The 8(a) program is not a 12-month path right now.** SBA approved no 8(a) applications between Aug 15, 2025 and at least Sept 2026, the new social-disadvantage rule took effect Sept 10, 2026, pending applications were returned for resubmission with a 45-day deadline, and SBA says it "cannot definitively estimate individual processing times." Only 65 firms were admitted under the current administration vs. ~800 terminations.
4. **Fast certifications exist:** SDVOSB (VetCert) now averages ~12 days (SBA, Nov 2025); HUBZone target 60 days; WOSB target 90 days. New York State and NYC allow non-competitive awards **up to $1.5M** to certified MWBE/SDVOB firms, which is the single largest documented "no-bid" door for a new firm in the country, if the owner qualifies.
5. **Realistic 12-month expectations for a capable full-time founder with an AI team and $0–10k:** ~30–40% chance of winning *something* federal/SLED; ~15–25% chance of booking $1M+ in total contract value by stacking 2–3 routes in parallel; ~5–10% chance of a single $1M+ prime award without a JV partner or a socioeconomic certification. Cash, not proposals, is the binding constraint on services contracts: a $500k staffing contract needs ~$80–120k of payroll float before the first government payment.

Reading: **two-thirds of $1–3M set-aside winners are firms with 20+ prior federal contracts.** The ~6–7% of awards going to zero-history entities are dominated by vehicles that import past performance (JVs, tribal/ANC subsidiaries) or by SBIR Phase II (which requires a prior Phase I). A truly independent new firm appears in perhaps 2–3% of $1M+ set-aside awards. Scaled to the first-timer population, that is on the order of a few hundred independent new firms per year nationwide landing a $1M+ federal prime.

Illustrative "staircase" case from the same data: **Global Impact Group LLC** (Raleigh, SDVOSB) won a $1.5M VA interpreter/translation IDIQ in March 2026 (SAM.gov https://sam.gov/opp/b0985425bdeb41ebb7bf8f136f4b0980/view). USAspending shows its first VA order in June 2017 was **$66**, followed by dozens of $100–$18k orders in 2017–2019. Nine years from a $66 micro-purchase to a $1.5M IDIQ is the typical shape, not the exception.

## 3. Fastest routes, with documented mechanics

### 3.1 Micro-purchases and simplified acquisitions (federal)
- Below $15k a cardholder can buy with no competition; $15k–$350k is reserved for small business (Rule of Two) with simplified procedures (FAR Part 13). CRS IF13123.
- These are where an AI team monitoring SAM.gov 24/7 adds the most leverage: speed of response, clean quotes, and responsiveness are the discriminators, not past performance. But **stacking $1M from SAT awards means 5–15 wins in 12 months**; my FY2025 sample shows 81% of new set-aside awards in services NAICS were under $1M.
- Caveat: SAM.gov posting is not required below $25k, and many SAT buys never hit SAM. Relationship work (calling contracting officers, small business specialists, OSDBUs) is what surfaces them.

### 3.2 New York's $1.5M non-competitive MWBE/SDVOB door (SLED)
- **NYC**: M/WBE Noncompetitive Small Purchase Method threshold raised to **$1.5M** effective Dec 19, 2023; agencies on average register these contracts at only **$325,387** (NYC Comptroller, Annual Report on M/WBE Procurement FY25 https://comptroller.nyc.gov/reports/annual-report-on-m-wbe-procurement-fy25/ ; compliance reports https://comptroller.nyc.gov/wp-content/uploads/documents/MWBE_Compliance_Report_Calendar_2025_Q1.pdf). NYC DOE describes the method as awards "without formal competition" https://infohub.nyced.org/in-our-schools/working-with-the-doe/contracts-and-purchasing/open-doe-solicitations/mwbe-procurement-and-contract-method
- **NYS**: FY2025–26 budget (Part KK) extended the MWBE program to July 1, 2028 and raised discretionary purchasing thresholds for MWBEs and SDVOBs from $750,000 to **$1.5M** (Hancock Estabrook https://www.hancocklaw.com/publications/mwbe-services-legal-alert-new-york-mwbe-program-extended-through-july-1-2028/ ; Governor's Nov 18, 2025 release https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-announces-record-setting-33-billion-state-spending-minority-and-women-owned).
- This is the largest documented single-award no-bid door available to a *new* certified firm anywhere in US public procurement. The constraints: eligibility (minority/woman/SDV ownership), 3–6 months to certify, and the buyer still has to *want* you; the $325k average shows agencies use the method for mid-size buys.
- Comparable (smaller) doors: California SB/DVBE Option $250k; most states have small-purchase or MBE preference thresholds in the $50k–$250k range.

### 3.3 Subcontracting and teaming
- Large primes with contracts > $950k must carry subcontracting plans (FAR 19.7; 52.219-9) and post opportunities on SBA SubNet. Subcontracts carried ~$94B to small firms in FY25 **[CALC: $273B total minus $179B prime, SBA FY25 release]**.
- Payment: primes must pay small business subs within 15 days of receiving accelerated government payment (FAR 52.232-40, current through FAC 2026-01) https://www.acquisition.gov/far/52.232-40.
- Subcontract past performance counts: 13 CFR 125.11 requires agencies to consider first-tier subcontractor and JV-member past performance.

### 3.4 Joint ventures (the documented year-one $1M+ pattern)
- 13 CFR 125.8(e): when evaluating a small-business JV, the agency "must consider work done and qualifications held individually by each partner to the joint venture as well as any work done by the joint venture itself." GAO enforces this (AttainX, B-421216, 2023) https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-13/chapter-I/part-125/section-125.8 ; https://natlawreview.com/article/gao-issues-noteworthy-protest-decision-joint-venture-experience
- Protégé/8(a) member must perform ≥40% of JV work.
- Evidence it works in year one: PrimeRFP's "America at 250" (first prime awards since Jan 1, 2024; all 250 listed first awards were ≥ $2.3M; medians ~$8.75M SDVOSB, ~$15M small-business primes) is dominated by JV entities (Blueway One JV $48.8M VA; Bridger Richard JV $47.3M VA; Amcor JV One $33.9M VA; Kre JV $66.4M DoD; Ahtna USA-CDM JV $39.2M) https://primerfp.com/blog/america-at-250-first-federal-contract-winners. My USAspending pull (§1.4) shows the same: half of zero-history $1M+ winners are JVs/affiliates.
- The catch: an established firm agrees to JV with a brand-new company only when the new company brings something (a certification the partner lacks, e.g., SDVOSB/HUBZone/WOSB status for a set-aside; a specific relationship; or labor the partner cannot staff).

### 3.5 DoD AI/data: Tradewinds and DIU
- **Tradewinds Solutions Marketplace** (CDAO): 4,509 solutions submitted, 1,559 "awardable" (35%); $5.41B awarded cumulative; **2025: $1.753B across 80 awards, 221 new entrants to DoW, median PALT 45 days** (tradewindai.com https://www.tradewindai.com/tw-marketplace). So in 2025 roughly 5% of awardable solutions converted to an award **[CALC: 80/1,559]**, and 2026 "awardable" announcements are dominated by established vendors (CACI, Databricks, C3 AI, Progress). "Awardable" status does not guarantee an award. The 88%-to-nontraditional figure circulating online is from a third-party consultancy, not CDAO **[VENDOR CLAIM]**.
- **DIU CSO**: 450 prototype OTs FY16–FY23, ~$1.7B obligated, 51% transition rate, but **average 172 days to award** (85% missed the 60–90-day goal) (GAO-25-106856 https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-25-106856/index.html). Startup-guide claims of $1–5M awards in 3–6 months are optimistic **[VENDOR]**.

### 3.6 SBIR/STTR (reauthorized April 13, 2026 through Sept 30, 2031)
- Standard Phase I/II ceilings unchanged by the bill; Phase I guideline ~$305k via SBA inflation adjustment; agency examples: NSF $305k, DOE $250k, NASA $225k, AFWERX open topic as low as $75k. New $30M "Strategic Breakthrough" tier is for post-Phase II firms. Per-firm proposal caps begin FY2027. https://precisionfederal.com/insights/sbir-2026-reauthorization-what-changed ; https://federalnewsnetwork.com/commentary/2026/04/the-sbir-restart-wont-be-easy/
- Selection rates: ~16% of Phase I proposals government-wide (FY2022 SBA data as compiled by consultants); DoD ~18%; NIH 8–13%; first-time applicants ~10–15% **[VENDOR compilations of SBA data]** https://www.getcada.com/insights/nsf-sbir-success-rates
- Phase I alone cannot reach $1M; Phase II ($1–2M) typically lands 12–24 months after Phase I. SBIR is a year-2/3 route to $1M+.

### 3.7 FEMA / disaster
- Stafford Act §307 and FAR 26.2 give preference to local firms via local-area set-asides or evaluation preference (FAC 2026-01) https://www.acquisition.gov/far/subpart-26.2 ; SAT rises to $1M/$2M in declared emergencies (CRS IF13123). No published data on new-vendor awards after 2024–2026 disasters. Debris and grant-management work goes overwhelmingly to pre-positioned firms with standby contracts; counties pre-award "FEMA grant management services" annual contracts (e.g., Charlotte County FL, Dec 2024) https://www.governmentcontracts.us/government-contracts/opportunity-details/26611592805118855.htm. Realistic for a new firm only as a local labor subcontractor.

### 3.8 VA
- VA Vets First: SDVOSB/VOSB set-asides are mandatory when the Rule of Two is met (Kingdomware); sole source to $5M (VAAR 819.70). VA is the deepest market for staffing (NAICS 561320: SDVOSB set-aside awards of $3–11M to firms like Arrow Arc, Prometheus Federal Services, SI2, Ansible Government Solutions in FY2025 **[USAspending pull]**), janitorial (561720: $6–60M), and language services. SDVOSB status is the gating asset.

## 5. Large private-sector contracts

- **No documented case was found** of a solo-founded, bootstrapped firm landing a single $1M+ enterprise contract in its first year via a pilot. The nearest documented patterns are SaaS firms reaching $1M ARR across many customers (e.g., Pocus: $6k first deal to $100k enterprise deals, $1M ARR in < 1 year) https://saasclub.io/podcast/first-customers/ ; a Forbes op-ed argues solo founders hit a soft ceiling of $1–3M ARR and lose enterprise deals to teams with a dedicated salesperson https://www.forbes.com/sites/nehamehra/2026/08/03/why-ai-agent-startups-are-becoming-the-new-solo-founder-playbook/ **[opinion]**.
- **Franchisor approved-supplier programs** exist (FDD Item 8 discloses supplier arrangements and rebates); some are pay-to-play (SERVPRO's program involves a percentage of sales, conference booth purchases, discounted pricing) https://www.servpro.com/about/preferred-vendor ; https://franchiseelawyer.com/blog/can-a-franchisor-require-franchisees-to-use-specific-vendors/. Approval typically requires samples, audits, financial-stability disclosures; it is a 6–18-month vetting cycle and rarely a guaranteed volume commitment.
- Honest read: a $1M enterprise contract to a brand-new vendor requires a warm executive relationship or a lighthouse pilot that converts; AI implementation pilots in 2026 are plentiful but usually $25–150k.

Defensibility of the probability numbers: they are judgment calls anchored on (a) ~6–7% of $1M+ set-aside awards going to zero-history entities, with roughly half of those being JVs/affiliates; (b) ~10–12k first-time winners per year and sub-10% of registrants winning anything; (c) CSIS/Baroni survival and entry data; (d) Tradewinds 80 awards per 1,559 awardables; (e) SBIR 10–16% selection. They are not drawn from any published cohort study, because none exists.

## 9. Defending the top 2 with a concrete first 60 days

### Route 1: Teaming → JV (the only documented year-one $1M+ pattern)

**Days 1–10: Be awardable.**
- Form the entity, EIN, SAM.gov registration (10 business days clean; pre-verify IRS legal name), CAGE, small-business self-certification in SAM, NAICS stack (561320, 561110, 541511/519, 541611, 541930 as fits). If the founder is a service-disabled veteran, file VetCert day 1 (12-day average). If woman-owned, file WOSB day 1 (≤90 days). If you can locate the principal office in a HUBZone and hire 35% HUBZone residents, decide now.
- AI team: build the capability statement (one page, NAICS, differentiators, key-personnel résumés doing the past-performance work), a past-performance narrative built from the founder's prior employment, and a pricing model with SCA wage determinations and a defensible wrap rate (Deltek: pricing defensibility is the #1 loss reason).

**Days 10–30: Build the partner target list, not the bid list.**
- AI team pulls from USAspending/FPDS every small business that won 3+ set-aside awards in your NAICS in your geography in FY2024–25 (the "21+ prior awards" cohort that wins the $1–3M band), and every large prime with a subcontracting plan that is behind on its SDVOSB/HUBZone/WOSB goals (SubNet + agency OSDBU reports). Rank by: (a) they lack a certification you hold or can get; (b) they are recompeting something in 6–12 months; (c) they have open SubNet postings.
- Founder: 15–20 live conversations with partner CEOs/BD leads (OSDBU matchmaking events, APEX Accelerator (formerly PTAC) intros, SAME/NCMA/AFCEA chapter meetings). The ask is specific: "I bring X (certification/labor pool/AI-drafted proposal capacity); you bring past performance; we JV under 13 CFR 125.8 on these three recompetes."

**Days 30–60: Paper it and get on a bid.**
- Sign 1–2 teaming agreements and one JV operating agreement that satisfies 13 CFR 125.8(b)-(c) (managing venturer = the small business, 40% work-share, separate bank account). If the partner is large, file the SBA Mentor-Protégé application (budget 105 days; you cannot bid as an MPP JV until approved).
- As a subcontractor in the meantime: get written past-performance letters on every task, however small, and register the JV in SAM.
- Target: by day 60, one signed JV, two subcontract task orders in progress, and a capture plan on 3 set-aside recompetes of $1–5M due in months 4–9.

### Route 2 (eligible founders): NYC/NYS MWBE or SDVOB discretionary purchases up to $1.5M

**Days 1–15:** Apply for NYC SBS M/WBE and NYS ESD MWBE (or SDVOB via OGS) certification simultaneously; the clocks (90–180 days) start on a *complete* package, so AI team assembles the full documentary file (ownership, control, tax returns, résumés, licenses) before submission. Register on the City's PASSPort and the State's NYS Contract Reporter / OGS vendor systems.

**Days 15–45:** AI team scrapes PASSPort and Contract Reporter for every award made under the M/WBE Noncompetitive Small Purchase Method and NYS discretionary authority in your service categories over the last 24 months (agency, buyer, amount, vendor). That is the buyer list. Founder books meetings with the agency Chief Contracting Officers and M/WBE officers at the 5–8 agencies that use the method most (DOE, DDC, DOT, H+H, OTI, HRA, state agencies with the highest MWBE utilization reports). Pitch a scoped $150–500k engagement that the buyer can award *without an RFP* once your certification lands.

**Days 45–60:** Pre-negotiate scope and price with two buyers so that the purchase order can be issued the week certification posts; line up milestone invoicing (30-day terms) and a factor or a partner with payroll capacity if the work needs staff. Target: certification in month 4–6, first discretionary award (~$300k) in month 5–8, second and third stacked to cross $1M by month 10–14.

### Why not lead with Route 3 (federal set-asides) or Route 5 (Tradewinds)?
Route 3 is where the money is ($32.5B SDVOSB primes) but 63% of $1–3M winners have 21+ prior awards, and staffing/janitorial awards need $80–120k of payroll float per $500k, which a $0–10k founder does not have without a partner (Route 1) or a factor. Route 5 requires a product, and 2025 produced 80 awards against 1,559 awardable solutions. Both are strong year-2 routes layered on top of Route 1.
