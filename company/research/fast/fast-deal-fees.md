# Fast Deal Fees: Which intermediation businesses can plausibly gross $1M in 12 months from $0-10k, no license, no audience, with an AI agent team?

Prepared 2026-10-21 for Medina Digital AI. Research method: web search (extended) plus direct fetches of statutes, SEC releases, SEC DERA data, industry reports and operator interviews. Every figure is tagged: **[primary]** (statute, regulator, court, filed data), **[industry survey]** (trade body or platform data with disclosed sample), **[self-reported]** (operator/podcast), or **[vendor]** (course seller, lead vendor, franchisor marketing). Promotional numbers are flagged as such.

---

## 0. The bottom line before the detail

1. **There is no verified, documented case of a true cold-start intermediary (no capital, no license, no prior industry relationships) grossing $1M in fees in their first 12 months in any of the eight categories.** The closest documented case is land flipper Pete Reese (~$1.2M revenue in his first partial year, 2021, ~50% gross margin) and he (a) had ~20 years as a licensed broker/investor, (b) bought with his own cash, and (c) self-reports on podcasts. [self-reported]
2. **The one category where brand-new firms demonstrably reached $1M+ of fee revenue within ~12-18 months is transferable clean-energy tax credit intermediation** (Basis Climate, founded 2022: "$700M+ transacted, 60+ deals" at 0.5-3% fees; Crux, founded 2023, now runs an SEC-registered broker-dealer). Both were venture-funded and founded by ex-finance operators, not solo $10k starters. [vendor/self-reported]
3. **Base rate for any new firm:** in JPMorgan Chase Institute's 835,000-firm panel, ~4% of small businesses that survived year one hit $1M revenue in year one; ~9% within five years; the authors say the true share is probably lower because some "new" firms pre-existed. [industry survey, primary data]
4. **Base rates for the specific trades:** first-two-years REALTORS median gross income $8,000 (62% under $10k) [NAR 2026 Member Profile]; new business brokers: 40-55% close zero deals in year one [weakly sourced to IBBA/BBP]; new equipment-finance brokers $30-100k year one [vendor]; scratch insurance agency $25-60k revenue year one [vendor].
5. **My probability estimates for this specific operator (full-time, capable, AI team, $0-10k) hitting $1M gross in 12 months:** best category 3%, worst under 0.5%. Hitting $3M in 24 months: 1-3%. Those are not pessimism; they are the base rate times the genuine AI advantage (lead generation and paperwork are the cost centers AI collapses; trust and buyer relationships are the bottleneck it does not).
6. **Ranked top 5:** (1) Land + commercial/multifamily wholesaling with novation, (2) Transferable tax-credit intermediation, (3) Lower-middle-market M&A sourcing/finder under the 2023 federal M&A broker exemption, (4) Residential wholesaling in permissive states, (5) Commercial loan / equipment-finance brokerage. Syndication capital-raising for a fee is excluded as an unregistered-broker violation. Insurance, government-contract brokering, yacht brokerage are excluded for licensing or statutory reasons detailed below.

## 1. Real estate wholesaling

### 1a. Residential wholesaling

**Fee data.**
- National average assignment fee ~$13,000; regional range ~$5,000 (AZ) to ~$22,000 (NC, GA). Source: Real Estate Bees survey of "1,000+ professional wholesalers," published Oct 2025. Methodology not published; treat as [industry survey, unverified]. https://realestatebees.com/statistics/is-wholesaling-real-estate-legal/
- Vendor datasets claim higher: Jaken Finance Group "~$18,500 national average, $45k+ for experienced metro operators" [vendor]; direct-mail-sourced deals median $15-20k [vendor]. https://ispeedtolead.com/blog/the-2026-real-estate-wholesaler-profit-margin-report-what-12000-investors-earned-by-lead-source/
- Deals per year for beginners: 2-6 in year one (course-seller blogs); "0.5-1 deals/month" beginner tier. [vendor] https://www.realestateskills.com/blog/wholesale-salary

**Math for a $1M/yr residential operator.** At the $13k average you need ~77 closings/year (6.4/month). At $20k (good markets, good negotiation) you need 50. At a blended $4,000 cost per contract that is $200-300k/year of marketing plus 3-5 acquisition staff. Operators at that level exist (they are the ones selling courses), but none documents reaching it in year one from zero capital. **No verified first-year $1M case found.** Claims such as a course founder's "50+ deals in my first year" are unverified [vendor]. The circulating "90% of wholesalers never close a deal" figure has no traceable source [vendor].

**Legal/licensing (residential), verified where possible.**
- **Pennsylvania, Act 52 of 2024 (SB 1173), effective Jan 4, 2025 [primary]:** amends the Real Estate Licensing and Registration Act so that anyone who "engages or attempts to engage in a wholesale transaction" falls within the definitions of "broker" and "salesperson" -- i.e., a license is required; adds mandatory disclosures and a consumer cancellation right. Act text (copy): https://go.stewart.com/rs/067-YWO-436/images/2024%20Act%2052%20-%20PA%20General%20Assembly.pdf ; PAR summary: https://parealtors.org/blog/gov-shapiro-signs-senate-bill-1173-into-law . Philadelphia separately requires a city wholesaler license.
- **South Carolina, H.4754 (2024):** marketing another's property for compensation is brokerage; licensees' firms barred from wholesaling; widely described as the most restrictive state. [secondary summaries of statute] https://realestatebees.com/statistics/is-wholesaling-real-estate-legal/
- **Oklahoma, SB 1075 (2025), effective Nov 1, 2025 [primary bill docs]:** defines "wholesaler" and "double closing" for residential real estate; requires written disclosure of intent to resell equitable interest at a higher price, advice to seek counsel, a short no-penalty cancellation window (2 business days in the final versions; verify enrolled text), bars liens/clouding title; missing disclosures void the contract. Public marketing of a contract you hold was already treated as licensed activity under HB 3046 (2022). https://oklegislature.gov/cf_pdf/2025-26%20COMMITTEE%20SUBS/HCSS/SB1075%20HCS.PDF ; https://www.virtualunderwriter.com/bulletins/2025/11/ok2025001
- **Illinois, 225 ILCS 454 (Public Act 101-0357):** more than one wholesale transaction in a 12-month period is a "pattern of business" requiring a broker license; a separate registration regime ($75/2 yrs with IDFPR) applies to those who wholesale regularly. [secondary; ilga.gov was unreachable during research] https://www.reifunnel.com/blog/wholesale-real-estate-compliance-laws
- Disclosure-only states include Texas (Prop. Code 1101.0045), Arizona, Ohio and others. Residential statutes above are residential-specific; **land and commercial wholesaling largely sits outside them**, though the older "marketing property you do not own = brokerage" doctrine still applies in every state, so the compliant move is to market the contract (equitable interest), not the property.
- Outreach law: SMS/cold-call blasts are TCPA-exposed; email is CAN-SPAM. Budget for compliant consent and DNC scrubbing.

### 1b. Land wholesaling / flipping

- **Documented operator: Pete Reese (Reelvest / Turning Profit).** First property resold March 2021; "that first year, partial year, we did about $1.2 million in revenue, and about 50% gross profit." 2022: ~$3.5M revenue, just under 50% gross; 2023: ~$8.2M. Direct mail cost ~$3,000-3,500 per deal; average profit ~$22-30k per deal. He had ~20 years as a licensed broker/investor and funded purchases with his own cash (flipping, not assigning). [self-reported, multiple podcasts] https://lifebridgecapital.com/2023/02/09/ws1572-getting-successful-in-land-flipping-business-pete-reese/ ; https://www.thelandgeek.com/pete-reese-shares-his-model-to-make-money-and-build-wealth/ ; https://www.realestateinvestingmastery.com/1195-the-details-on-how-pete-is-averaging-500k-per-month-flipping-vacant-land/
- Course-seller claims for land wholesaling (assign, do not buy): "$5-25k per deal with $0 down" [vendor]; student testimonials "$80k in first 6 months" [vendor, unverified]. https://landydandy.com/wholesale-land-flipping
- Why land fits an AI team: owners are often absentee/out-of-state, lists are public (assessor + GIS), comps are data problems, there is no occupant and no repairs, and response rates to neutral letters (1-4%) are higher than houses. Competition has risen sharply since 2021 (the Reese cohort), so expect worse unit economics than his.
- Without capital you assign or double-close with transactional funding (1-2 points), which caps you at ~$5-25k/deal; the $22-30k/deal figures assume buying at 40-50% of value with your own cash.

### 1c. Commercial and multifamily wholesaling, and novation

- Fee claims: $25,000 to six figures, occasionally seven, with 60-90+ day diligence; buyers are syndicators, family offices, REITs. [vendor] https://www.commercialcafe.com/blog/how-to-wholesale-commercial-real-estate/ ; a podcast series on "6-figure wholesaling checks" exists but the transcript page was unavailable; treat six-figure fees as plausible-but-undocumented for a first-year operator. https://www.realestateinvestingmastery.com/1341-6-figure-wholesaling-checks-with-trent-ellingford/
- **No documented $100k+ commercial assignment by a first-year operator was found.**
- Novation (seller lists with a licensed agent at retail; the "wholesaler" is paid outside closing as a contractual party) keeps the fee off the HUD and works for retail-condition houses; the risk is that payment is outside escrow. [vendor explainers] https://retipster.com/terms/novation/ ; https://www.vahorizon.site/guides/novation-wholesaling-real-estate/

### 1d. What AI automates here
List-building and scoring (assessor, GIS, tax-delinquency, probate, code violations, loan-maturity data), skip-tracing, first-touch personalized mail/email copy, inbound triage, comps and offer generation, contract drafting, buyer-list building from deed records, disposition emails. What it does not do: the seller phone call that converts (still human, ~1-2 hours per contract), title/closing coordination, and buyer trust.

## 2. Real estate syndication and capital raising

**Fees [industry explainers, consistent across law firms and sponsors]:** acquisition fee 1-3% of purchase price (2-3% common; up to 5% on small deals), asset-management fee 1-2% of gross revenue or 0.5-1.5% of equity, promote/carry 20-30% over a 7-8% preferred return. https://www.cre.law/simplifying-sponsor-fees-and-equity-in-real-estate-syndication/ ; https://www.moschettilaw.com/real-estate-syndications-fees-and-equity/

**Time to first deal:** practitioner guides put a first-time GP at 12-24 months from serious start to first close; "successful GPs spent 12-18 months building a list of qualified investors before going under contract." [vendor/practitioner] https://rodkhleif.com/what-are-the-first-steps-to-becoming-a-multifamily-syndicator/ . First raises of $3-7M for value-add deals are described as feasible with mentorship; development is not.

**Market size [primary, SEC DERA]:** 2025 Reg D: 34,553 initial offerings, $2.39 trillion sold; 17,593 pooled-fund issuers ($2.12T) vs 16,960 non-fund issuers ($273B); 506(b) 30,315 offerings, 506(c) 3,989. https://www.sec.gov/data-research/statistics-data-visualizations/regulation-d-offerings

**The real risk: unregistered broker-dealer activity.** 
- Three-factor test used by syndication counsel: (1) you introduce investors, (2) you are paid on a transaction basis (percent of capital raised, per-close fee), (3) you do it with regularity. All three present = broker, whatever you call it ("co-GP," "marketing fee," "finder's fee"). Consequences: investor rescission rights for the whole offering, sponsor liability, SEC penalties. [law-firm analysis] https://syndicationattorneys.com/articles/paying-a-finder-can-invalidate-your-entire-raise-heres-the-rule-youre-breaking/
- **SEC v. StraightPath sales agents (Press Release 2024-127, Sept 12, 2024) [primary]:** three unregistered agents sold LLC interests, gave marketing materials, advised on merits, took ~$2.1M transaction-based comp on ~$17M raised from 75+ investors. Sanctions: disgorgement + interest of $431k / $1.39M / $544k, civil penalties $100k / $300k / $90k, industry bars. https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024-127
- *SEC v. Kramer*, 778 F. Supp. 2d 1320 (M.D. Fla. 2011) [primary]: the court refused to treat transaction-based comp alone as dispositive and found Kramer a finder because he did not negotiate, analyze or advise -- but it is a single district-court outlier and the SEC's 2020 proposed finder exemption was never adopted. https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2183367/sec-v-kramer/ ; https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2020/11/05/safe-harbor-for-permissible-capital-raising-activities-by-unregistered-finders/
- Compliant structures: (a) register with a broker-dealer (Series 7/63/82, months of time); (b) genuine co-GP with substantive ongoing duties and compensation not tied to dollars raised; (c) fund-of-funds / feeder fund where you are the manager of your own vehicle and earn management fees and carry on your fund (Tribevest, Avestor sell the infrastructure; still needs securities counsel and a Form D). https://www.crowdfundinglawyers.net/feeder-funds-real-estate-syndications/
- **Verdict for this operator:** a first-time GP with $0-10k and no track record cannot raise institutional-size equity in 12 months; a capital-raiser taking a percentage is committing a federal violation. Fee math: $1M of acquisition fees at 2% requires $50M of closed acquisitions in year one. Probability of $1M gross in 12 months: under 0.5%. Not ranked.

## 3. Business brokerage and M&A finder fees

**Fee data.**
- Firmex/Axial M&A Fee Guide 2024-25 [industry survey of advisors]: average success fee ~4.8% on a $5M deal, ~3.4% at $20M, ~2.0% at $100M; typical retainers $5-10k/month, often credited against the success fee; Lehman/modified Lehman structures common. https://www.firmex.com/resources/reports-guides/us-fee-guide-25/ ; https://4804905.fs1.hubspotusercontent-eu1.net/hubfs/4804905/Firmex-MAFeeGuide-Global-25.pdf
- Main Street: 8-15% of price with minimums (IBBA-cited) [industry]; BizBuySell 2025 median sale price $350,000, 94% of asking, median 170 days to close [platform data]. https://www.bizbuysell.com/blog/2025-year-in-review/
- IBBA/M&A Source Market Pulse Q4 2025 (350 respondents): individual buyers were 44% of lower-middle-market acquisitions; 90% of sell-side clients are first-time sellers. [industry survey] https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/the-ibba-and-ma-source-announce-the-market-pulse-q4-2025-survey-results-302691992.html

**First-year brokers.**
- A 2013 Business Brokerage Press poll: a new broker "should" earn ~$47k in year one (opinion, not measured). [industry]
- CT Acquisitions (recruits brokers, so [vendor]) claims 40-55% of new brokers close zero deals in the first 12 months and exit before month 24; median Main Street listing-to-close 7-10 months; 25-35% of listings close. Attributed loosely to IBBA/BBP; not verifiable from the Market Pulse. https://ctacquisitions.com/become-a-business-broker-career-and-licensing/
- Franchise brokerage year-one gross commissions estimated $30-80k [vendor]. **No documented first-year $1M broker found.**

**Legality.**
- **Federal: Exchange Act §15(b)(13), effective March 29, 2023 [primary]:** no SEC registration needed for brokering the sale of an "eligible privately held company" (EBITDA < $25M or revenue < $250M in the prior fiscal year) where the buyer will control the company (presumed at 25%+ voting/capital) and be active in management. Lose the exemption if you take custody of funds, provide financing, bind a party, deal with passive buyers or shell companies, or represent both sides without written consent. **Does not preempt state law.** https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2023/03/us-congress-exempts-certain-merger-and-acquisition-brokers-from-sec-broker-dealer-registration
- **States:** roughly 21-23 have adopted a NASAA-style M&A broker exemption (adoption lists vary by source; NASAA model rule 2024). **New York** relies on transaction-specific exemptions under GBL §359-f, not a parallel M&A broker rule. **California:** business-opportunity brokerage is typically done by a DRE-licensed real estate broker or a FINRA BD; CA also expects direct filings. **Texas:** no business-broker license, but a real estate license is generally needed to collect a commission when real estate or a lease assignment is in the deal. **Florida:** "real estate" is defined to include business enterprises, so a Florida real estate license is generally required unless FINRA-registered. Under Exchange Act §29(b) and state blue-sky analogues, contracts made in violation are void and fees are clawback-exposed; courts are reluctant to award quantum meruit (SDCBA 2015). https://www.ibba.org/articles/the-state-line-trap/ ; https://darroweverett.com/sec-broker-exemption-mergers-acquistions-rules-analysis/ ; https://www.finalis.com/blog/m-a-broker-exemption
- Asset sales of an LLC's assets (not equity) are generally outside securities law entirely; equity sales are inside it. Structure accordingly.

**Where the AI edge is real:** proprietary deal origination. Buy-side mandates (search funds, independent sponsors, PE add-on platforms) pay 1-3% or Lehman-type finder fees for proprietary introductions; Axial and similar networks are where these buyers live. AI can build owner lists (NAICS + age + tenure + UCC filings + license renewals), write genuinely personalized letters, and pre-qualify. The cycle (7-10 months to close) means year-one revenue depends on deals signed in months 1-3.

**Math:** $1M = one $25M deal at 4%, or three $8M deals at ~4.3%, or ~30 Main Street deals at $35k. Probability of $1M gross in 12 months: ~2%; $3M by month 24: ~3% (the pipeline you build in year one pays in year two).

## 4. Commercial loan, SBA and equipment-finance brokering

**Fees.**
- SBA 7(a): Form 159 [primary] requires every compensated agent to disclose; lenders cannot charge referral/commission fees to applicants; agent compensation must bear "a necessary and reasonable relationship to the services actually performed"; SOP 50 10 8 requires itemization above $2,500 aggregate; contingency-only fees are barred. Referral-only fees are typically 0.5-1.5% of funded amount, often capped around $3,000 (industry practice, not regulation). https://www.sba7a.loans/downloads/SBA-form-159-official.pdf ; https://gosbaloans.com/guide/sba-broker-agent-fee-rules/
- Equipment finance: 2-5% of financed amount is the most-cited band (some vendors claim 10-15%); paid at funding; clawbacks on early default. Commercial mortgage: ~1% origination. MCA ISOs: 8-12 points (vendor claims up to 14-19) on advances with APRs that make this a reputational and regulatory liability; excluded. https://www.equipmentfinanceacademy.com/blog/how-much-do-equipment-finance-brokers-make-salary-commission-2026 ; https://jyni.io/blog/mca-broker-commission-rates
- First-year income: $30-100k (vendor training programs); illustrative model of 3 funded deals/month at $50k average nets ~$50k year one. [vendor]

**Licensing.**
- California: brokering loans secured by real property requires a DRE broker license (B&P §10131); brokering to CFL lenders requires a CFL broker license ($25k bond, net-worth test). **AB 2116 (signed Sept 30, 2026) [primary]** adds a DFPI license for commercial financing brokers arranging $500k-or-less financings to small businesses, operative July 1, 2028. https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/california-enacts-commercial-financing-licensing-requirement
- Commercial financing disclosure laws (CA, NY, UT, VA, GA, FL, CT, KS) impose disclosure duties on providers and in some cases brokers; many states require a mortgage-broker license for real-estate-secured commercial loans. Unsecured business-loan brokering is largely unlicensed outside CA/NY-type regimes.

**Math:** $1M at ~1.5 blended points = ~$67M of funded loans in 12 months; at 3 points on equipment, ~$33M. A productive solo broker funds $5-15M in year one. Probability of $1M in 12 months: ~1%. Ranked 5th only because it is lawful, fast to start, and AI collapses the sourcing cost (UCC-filing data, new-business registrations, equipment-heavy NAICS codes).

## 5. Transferable clean-energy tax credits (IRC §6418)

**Market [Crux market intelligence, platform data; treat as industry survey with a commercial interest].**
- 2025 transfer market ~$42B (up ~48% from ~$28B in 2024; ~$4B in 2023 per secondary sources); total monetization incl. tax equity/hybrids $63B; 2026 forecast $64-69.5B total monetization, ~$47.5-49B transfers. https://www.crux.com/insights/transferable-tax-credits ; https://www.pv-tech.org/us-clean-energy-finance-matured-in-2025-despite-policy-tightening-says-crux/
- Pricing 2025: investment-grade ITCs $0.92-0.95, PTCs $0.92-0.96; non-IG ITCs as low as ~$0.90; deals under $20M averaged ~$0.90 vs ~$0.935 for $50-100M+; sub-$5M deals ~$0.86 (ITC) [secondary citing Crux]. Buyers bid on 2-3x their target capacity; deals facilitated by Crux close in ~3 months on average. https://www.cenetcapital.com/blog/tax-credit-transfer-market-statistics
- **Fees: 0.5-3.0% of credit value, typically paid by the seller**; Crux's worked example uses 1.25% intermediary fee plus 2.75% insurance; insurance premiums 2-5% (1.5-3% above $10M, 2-5% below); seller usually covers buyer's diligence costs up to a cap (~$50k example). Reunion: quoted prices are "all in" and broker cost is borne by the seller. https://www.crux.com/insights/transferable-tax-credits ; https://www.reunioninfra.com/insights/frequently-asked-questions-about-tax-credit-transfers
- Who: sellers are developers, manufacturers (45X), carbon-capture and fuel producers; buyers are corporates with federal liability (1 in 4 Fortune 1000 participated in 2025); individuals are mostly shut out by passive-activity rules. "Thousands" of buyers, developers and intermediaries; Crux does not publish an intermediary count. Only 11% of sub-$10M deals were insured vs 67% of $10M+ deals; small sellers often use parent indemnities (55% of 2024 deals). https://www.crux.com/blog/4-ways-developers-can-prepare-to-sell-smaller-tax-credits

**Policy risk (OBBBA, signed July 4, 2025) [law-firm summaries of statute]:** §6418 transferability preserved; wind/solar (45Y/48E) lose credits for projects that begin construction after July 4, 2026 and are placed in service after Dec 31, 2027; storage, 45X manufacturing, 45Q, 45Z (extended to 2029), nuclear and geothermal continue; prohibited-foreign-entity buyer restrictions added from 2026. Net effect: a multi-year runway of already-safe-harbored projects plus non-solar credits; demand softened as OBBBA cut corporate liabilities 20-30% [Crux]. https://www.kirkland.com/publications/kirkland-alert/2025/08/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-brings-big-changes-to-green-energy-tax-credits ; https://seia.org/research-resources/clean-energy-provisions-big-beautiful-bill/

**Legal constraints on a new intermediary.**
- Tax law: final §6418 regulations distinguish brokers (fine; title never passes to you) from dealers (barred by the no-second-transfer rule). No federal license to broker. https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/clean-energy-tax-credit-transferability-guidance-issued
- Securities law: unsettled in public sources. Market leaders hedge: Crux runs "Crux Securities," an SEC-registered broker-dealer serving institutional counterparties; Baker Tilly brokers through Baker Tilly Capital (FINRA BD). A transaction-based fee for placing what a regulator could characterize as an investment contract is the same risk profile as Section 2 above. **Mitigation:** fixed-fee seller-side advisory (packaging, diligence, pricing, buyer outreach) rather than percent-of-close; or partner with a registered BD as an introducing source; get a written opinion before taking a contingent fee.
- Platforms: Crux, Reunion, Basis Climate, Atheva, Ever.green, Concentro. Anyone can list; the scarce asset is buyer mandates from tax departments, not sellers.

**Documented first-year $1M+:** Basis Climate (founded 2022) reports $700M+ transacted across 60+ deals; at 1-2% that is $7-14M of lifetime fees; its smallest deal was $600k. [self-reported company site] https://www.buildwithbasis.com/ . Crux raised venture capital and reports first-half-2025 market volume of $20B+. No solo-operator first-year cases exist in public sources.

**Math:** $1M at 1.5% = ~$67M of credits intermediated; at 1% on larger deals, $100M. A single mid-cap buyer mandate can absorb $10-50M/yr; one 100 MW solar project yields a $40-60M ITC. Probability of $1M in 12 months: ~3%; $3M in 24: ~2% (the OBBBA phaseouts shrink the 2027+ pipeline for solar/wind).

## 6. Insurance brokerage (commercial lines, benefits)

- Licensing: 2-12 weeks in most states; 39 states inside four weeks; 18 states require pre-licensing hours (e.g., Illinois 20 hours per line); Texas has no pre-licensing for most lines. [vendor/course providers] https://justinsuranceco.com/how-long-to-get-insurance-license
- Commissions: carrier disclosures average ~13.6% commercial auto, 16.2% commercial property, 13.5% commercial liability (range ~3-26%); new business pays more than renewals. [industry compilation] https://www.compensationresources.com/insights/insurance-producer-compensation-0825/
- Scratch agency year one: 50-150 policies, $25-60k revenue; direct carrier appointments require $200-500k written premium, so new agencies join aggregators (SIAA, Smart Choice) at 80/20-90/10 splits; profitability in 12-24 months. [vendor] https://insuranceproagencies.com/starting-insurance-agency-from-scratch
- First-year producer income $45-75k [vendor]. $1M of commission = ~$7-10M of placed premium in year one with no carrier appointments. **No documented case; probability under 0.5%.** Benefits (group health) pays 3-6% of premium plus PEPM fees and has the same appointment problem.

## 8. Base rates

- **JPMorgan Chase Institute, "Scaling to $1 Million" (2024) [industry survey on 835,000 firms' bank data, 2011-2020]:** ~9% of small businesses reached $1M annual revenue within five years; ~4% did so in year one; firms with low initial revenue rarely get there; the sample excludes firms that closed in year one and the authors note prior-existing firms bias the figure upward. https://www.jpmorganchase.com/content/dam/jpmc/jpmorgan-chase-and-co/institute/pdf/1-million-annual-revenues-as-a-small-business-milestone.pdf

- Share of all U.S. business units with $1M+ receipts: under 5% (Census SUSB/NES harmonized; blog derivation); JPMC: 0.2% of NES-D firms and 4.7% of Chase-banked firms had 2019 revenue of $1M+. https://www.jpmorganchase.com/... (above)

- Business brokers: 40-55% zero deals in year one [weak]; "should earn $47k" (2013 poll). Equipment finance brokers $30-100k year one [vendor]. Scratch insurance agency $25-60k [vendor]. Wholesalers: no credible failure survey exists; first-year 2-6 deals [vendor].
- SEC Reg D 2025: 34,553 offerings; median offering ~$2.5M; 506(c) general-solicitation offerings are only 3,989 of them. [primary]

**Interpretation:** across every category the median first-year outcome is five figures, the top decile is low six figures, and the documented seven-figure first years belong to people who brought capital, a license, prior relationships, or venture funding. An AI team changes the cost of the top of the funnel (research, lists, personalization, paperwork) by 5-10x; it does not change the conversion step that depends on a counterparty trusting a stranger with a large transaction.

## 10. First 60 days: top 2

### A. Land + commercial/multifamily wholesaling

**Days 1-7: setup.** Form the LLC. Pick 3 counties in disclosure-only states with absentee land ownership and active builder demand (candidates: TX, FL, GA, NC, TN, AZ; verify each state's current rule, exclude PA/SC/OK-public-marketing). AI pulls assessor + GIS data: vacant parcels 1-40 acres, owned 10+ years, out-of-state or out-of-county mailing address, tax-delinquent flag, road frontage, no floodplain. Score ~20,000 parcels; skip-trace the top 6,000 (~$0.12/record). Draft: neutral offer letter, seller FAQ, purchase agreement with assignment clause and 60-90 day close, assignment agreement, double-close instructions, disclosure addenda per state. Line up a transactional funder and a title company that has closed assignments.

**Days 8-21: outreach.** Mail 5,000 letters ($3,500-4,000). AI-personalized email to every address it can match, compliant SMS only with consent capture. Build the buyer list in parallel: AI scrapes county deed records for cash land buyers in the last 18 months, builder permit pullers, and land-investor Facebook/LandWatch posters; 300-500 names; a short survey email asks what they want (acreage, price band, counties). Also start the commercial track: AI screens multifamily 5-50 units with loan maturities in the next 12 months (county recordings), code violations, and tax liens; 1,500 owners; mail + email a direct offer letter.

**Days 22-45: convert.** Expect 50-150 land responses (1-3%). AI triages, pulls comps (recent sold vacant parcels within 5 miles), proposes offers at 40-60% of market. Owner handles 30-60 calls. Target 4-8 signed contracts. Market the contracts (not the property) to the buyer list at a $10-25k spread; where retail is the better exit, use novation with a licensed agent. Commercial: expect 10-25 responses; underwrite with NOI/cap-rate; 1-2 LOIs.

**Days 46-60: close.** First 1-2 land assignments ($10-25k each). Reinvest into 10,000 pieces/month. Set month-3 target of 3-4 closings/month, month-6 target of 6-8 land plus first commercial assignment ($50k+). Year-one realistic: $150-400k gross; upside case with 2 commercial fees: $600k-1M.

Kill criteria: under 1% response after 10,000 pieces, or under 1 contract per 40 responses by day 60, switch counties or exit.

### B. Transferable tax-credit intermediation

**Days 1-14: legal structure and positioning.** Engage securities counsel for a written opinion on (a) fixed-fee seller advisory, (b) contingent fee with a BD partner. Decide: start fixed-fee ($15-50k per engagement for packaging/pricing/buyer outreach) and migrate to contingent once the BD relationship exists. Learn the stack: §6418 final regs, IRS pre-filing registration portal, Reunion's TCTA term guide, Crux market data, insurance-broker contacts (2-3 brokers who write tax-credit policies). Build AI-generated collateral: a 6-page buyer memo ("how to cut 2026 federal liability by 7-9%"), a seller readiness checklist, a pricing model by credit type/size/insurance.

**Days 15-35: buyer origination (the scarce side).** AI screens 10-K/10-Q XBRL for companies with $5-150M current federal income-tax expense, no prior §6418 purchases (text search), profitable three years running; ~800 names. Personalized outreach to VP Tax / Tax Director / CFO: specific liability figure from their filing, savings at $0.90-0.93, timeline before Q4 estimated payments. Target 2-3 signed non-exclusive buyer mandates (fee from seller side; buyer pays nothing). Also contact 20 regional CPA firms and wealth managers as referral channels.

**Days 15-35, in parallel: seller origination.** AI mines EIA-860/860M, state interconnection queues, county building permits and USDA REAP awards for projects placed in service 2025-2026 by developers with 1-5 projects (credits $1-15M; underserved, 2-3 bids each, often uninsured). Outreach to CFO/founder with a pricing indication and a packaging offer.

**Days 36-60: first match.** Take 1-2 seller engagements, prepare the package (cost-seg, eligibility memo via counsel, prevailing-wage/apprenticeship documentation, PFE compliance rep), run a short bid among your mandated buyers plus platform listing, negotiate TCTA with counsel. Expect first close in month 4-6 (3-month cycle). Fee on a $10M credit at 1.25% = $125k; fixed-fee alternative $25-40k. Year-one realistic: 0-3 closings, $50-350k; upside with one $40M+ credit: $500k-1M.

Kill criteria: no buyer mandate by day 60, or counsel advises contingent fees are unworkable without BD registration and no BD will partner.

## 11. Decision guidance for the Owner

- The honest prior for "$1M gross in 12 months" is 1-3% in the best categories; the honest median is $50-200k. The plan should be built to survive the median and keep the option on the tail.
- Run (A) as the cash engine from day 1 and (B) as the one-big-check option in parallel; both are lawful without a license if structured as above; (3) M&A sourcing becomes the year-two layer once (A) has produced relationships with buyers who also buy businesses.
- Never take a percent of capital raised for a syndicator. Never contingent-fee a federal contract. Never broker equity sales in NY/CA/FL/TX without checking the state exemption. Have counsel opine before any contingent tax-credit fee.
- Review this document against actual results at day 60 and reprice the probabilities.
