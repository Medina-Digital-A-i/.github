# Fast International: Cross-Border Plays for a $0–10k, No-License US Operator with an AI Agent Team

**Prepared:** 21 October 2026
**Question:** Which international / cross-border plays have documented cases of $1M+ gross income within 12–24 months on little capital, and what are the real odds for a US-based individual with $0–10k, no license, a 24/7 multilingual AI agent team, and (case A) Spanish fluency or (case B) no Spanish?

---

## 0. Bottom line first

1. **There is no publicly documented case** of a solo, zero-license, sub-$10k operator in any of the nine categories below going from zero to $1M+ gross in 12–24 months that I could verify with a primary source. Every "$1M in a year" story in these spaces is either (a) a firm with a license, capital, or an existing book; (b) gross revenue that is mostly pass-through (staffing payroll, commodity invoice value); or (c) a promotional claim. Treat anyone selling you a course that says otherwise as the product.

2. **What *is* documented is the fee per deal.** Government-published or offering-document fee schedules make the arithmetic real in three places: Caribbean citizenship-by-investment (CBI) agent commissions of US$25,000–$50,000 per approved applicant, paid by the government ([IMI Daily, Jan 2026](https://www.imidaily.com/analysis/what-governments-pay-cbi-agents-in-commission/)); EB-5 referral commissions of ~$35,000 per investor in SEC enforcement records ([SEC 2015-274](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2015-274)); and EOR/staffing partner programs paying 10–20% recurring revenue share ([Remote](https://remote.com/en-us/partners/affiliates), [Oyster](https://oysterhr.com/affiliates)) or $1,500 per closed customer ([Deel](https://www.deel.com/affiliates/)). The gap between "fee per deal is real" and "you will close 20–40 of them in 24 months" is where almost everyone dies.

3. **Base rates.** Fewer than 1% of US nonemployer firms ever reach $1M in receipts (~58,000 of ~28 million in 2021, per Census data summarized by [Business Insider](https://businessinsider.nl/more-and-more-solo-entrepreneurs-are-raking-in-1-million-here-are-the-industries-where-business-is-booming/)). MANA's survey of manufacturers' rep firms 1–3 years old shows average gross commissions of ~$132,000 on ~$1.76M of sales ([MANA via ReferenceForBusiness](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/encyclopedia/Man-Mix/Manufacturers-Representatives.html)). Of 4,970 Realtors NAR surveyed in 2026, only 381 had closed even one international buyer ([RISMedia](https://www.rismedia.com/2026/07/29/home-purchases-foreign-buyers-decrease-nearly-20-percent/)). Those are the honest priors.

4. **Ranked top 5 with my probability of $1M+ GROSS within 24 months** (and net in parentheses):

| Rank | Play | P($1M gross, 24 mo) | P($1M net) | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | LatAm talent + AI delivery for US SMBs (staffing / EOR-partner / managed services) | **8–12%** | 1–2% | Gross is pass-through payroll; 25–35 seats billed at ~$3–4k/mo = $1M run-rate; Spanish is a real edge; AI does 70% of the sourcing and screening |
| 2 | Investor-migration intermediary (Caribbean CBI international marketing agent + US E-2/EB-5 referral via a registered broker-dealer) | **3–5%** | 2–3% | $25–50k per approved file is government-published; 20–40 files = $1M; but HNW access, agent registration, and a 2028 EU sunset cap it |
| 3 | Export / nearshoring commission agent (US goods into Mexico/LatAm; Mexican/CAFTA-DR makers into US buyers leaving Asia) | **2–4%** | 1–2% | 5–10% commissions require $10–20M of product flowing through you; MANA data says year-2 reality is ~$130k |
| 4 | Tariff-refund and trade-compliance services (IEEPA refund prep at 4–8% contingency; USMCA qualification; HTS work) | **2–3%** (decaying) | 1–2% | ~$35B of IEEPA refunds in process; small importers under-served; but only IOR or licensed customs broker can file; window is closing |
| 5 | Cross-border real estate referral / developer sales (Mexico, Costa Rica, DR, Portugal) | **1–2%** | <1% | $5–10k per closed referral; needs ~150 closings; US side needs a license outside California's finder exception |

Everything else (import arbitrage, commodity/surplus brokerage, development-bank procurement, standalone remittance/diaspora plays) is below 1% for this profile and I explain why.

5. **What to actually do:** Play 1 is the only one where the AI team is the product rather than a research assistant, and the only one where $1M *gross* is a volume problem rather than an access problem. Play 2 has the highest fee per deal and is the right second bet **if** the Owner can get in front of high-net-worth Latin Americans; otherwise skip it. The 60-day plans for both are in Section 4.

## 2. The nine plays, one by one

Each section covers: mechanics, real numbers, time to first deal, legal, capital, what AI automates, named operators, Spanish vs no-Spanish, and verdict.

### 2.1 Export agent / export management company (EMC)

**Mechanics.** You represent a US manufacturer abroad (or a foreign manufacturer in the US) on commission. Agent model: manufacturer keeps title, credit risk and pricing; you get a percentage on invoiced sales. Distributor/EMC buy-sell model: you take title and margin (needs capital, so out of scope).

**Real numbers.**
- Commissions: 5% is the classic benchmark ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/living/hiring-the-perfect-sales-rep-for-your-export-business/288062)); 1–5% for commodity-like goods, 10%+ in some fields ([ExportImportPractical](https://exportimportpractical.com/start-as-an-export-import-agent/)); 10–15% for manufactured goods, 7–10% commodities per an EMC guide ([DocShipper](https://docshipper.com/glossary/export-management-company-definition-logistics/), promotional). Manufacturers' reps generally: 7–15% ([Xoxoday](https://blog.xoxoday.com/compass/manufacturers-sales-rep-commission-rates/)).
- **Earnings base rate (the only hard datapoint):** MANA survey, rep firms 1–3 years old: ~$1.76M sales, ~$132,300 gross commissions. Established corporate rep firms: ~$10.3M sales, ~$714k commissions ([MANA data](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/encyclopedia/Man-Mix/Manufacturers-Representatives.html)). MANA members cite *declining* commission rates as a top challenge ([MANA](https://www.manaonline.org/news/the-biggest-challenge)).
- To gross $1M at 7.5% you need **$13.3M of product moving through your lines** in the year. A new agent with no track record typically signs 3–8 principals and takes 6–12 months to first commissioned shipment.

**Time to first deal:** 4–9 months is normal for industrial goods (quote → sample → trial order → repeat). Food/consumer goods into Mexican distributors can be faster (2–4 months) if the product already has FDA/COFEPRIS-compatible labeling.

**Legal.** No license. You must screen counterparties against BIS/OFAC lists, stay out of ITAR items, and comply with FCPA (commission agents in Latin America are a classic FCPA vector). Written agency agreement with territory, exclusivity, commission trigger (on payment, not on order), and tail. EXIM Express export credit insurance exists for small exporters (new-to-export, 95% coverage, premiums ~$0.55–$0.65 per $100 for 1–60 day terms per [EXIM 2026 chart](https://grow.exim.gov/hubfs/resources/EXIM-ECI-Policy-Comparison-Chart-2026-PRINT.pdf)) but it's the *exporter's* policy, which you can broker into the deal to unlock open-account terms.

**Capital:** $2–5k (LLC, samples, one trade-show trip, Gold Key if the principal pays it). Government help now costs full price (Section 1).

**Hot corridors given tariffs (2026):**
- *US → Mexico ag and food ingredients:* record $30.6B; Mexican importers are structurally short of US corn, pork, dairy, poultry ([USDA ERS](https://ers.usda.gov/data-products/chart-gallery/112986)). Grain is traded by majors; your niche is packaged/branded food, ingredients, equipment, and ag-tech into Mexican distributors.
- *US → Mexico industrial inputs for the IMMEX/maquila base:* Mexico's manufacturing boom imports US machinery, components, MRO.
- *Mexico/CAFTA-DR → US:* USMCA-qualifying goods pay 0% while Asia pays 10–12.5% plus 232 where applicable. Apparel (Guatemala, Honduras, El Salvador, DR), furniture, metal fabrication, plastics, automotive tier 2–3. This is where a Spanish-speaking agent representing *Mexican/Central American factories* to US buyers is most valuable.
- *Vietnam/India → US:* share gainers but 12.5% (VN) and crowded with established sourcing agents; no Spanish advantage.
- *Middle East / Africa:* US capital goods and food; long cycles, FCPA exposure, letters of credit; not a 12–24-month play.

**What AI automates (~60%):** target-list building from Comtrade/USITC DataWeb/Census trade data by HS code; distributor identification; multilingual outreach sequences; product sheet translation; HTS classification and USMCA rules-of-origin pre-screens; proposal and agency-agreement drafting; CRM. **What it cannot do:** the factory visit, the plant manager's trust, the trade show handshake, the credit-risk judgment.

**Named operators:** Shelter providers like Tetakawi, Prodensa, NAPS, IVEMSA, Tecma, CPI, American Industries, Entrada are the documented *firms* in this corridor, but they are operations outsourcers, not commission agents ([Nearshore Navigator pricing matrix, Sept 2026](https://nearshorenavigator.com/en/insights/top-10-shelter-companies-in-mexico-2026-matrix)). Cosmo Sourcing sells Mexico sourcing on a fixed fee; most Mexico sourcing agents charge 3–10% ([Woosourcing](https://woosourcing.com/sourcing-agent-fees-commission-structure/); [FindSourcingAgents](https://findsourcingagents.com/blog/sourcing-from-mexico-guide-2026)). **I found no verifiable first-year $1M export agent.**

**Spanish vs no Spanish:** Spanish is close to mandatory for the Mexico/Central America corridor at the plant-manager level; without it you're limited to the English-speaking export managers of larger firms, or to Vietnam/India (where you have no edge).

**Verdict:** Real, legal, cheap, and slow. Realistic year-2 outcome: $80–250k commissions. P($1M gross in 24 mo) ≈ 2–4%.

### 2.2 Nearshoring brokerage (US companies leaving China → Mexico / Central America / Colombia)

**Mechanics and who pays whom.** Three money flows exist: (a) the shelter provider's per-employee fee ($160–$350/employee/month, or 10–15% on direct payroll, or $8.50–$14/sq ft bundled campus leases) ([Nearshore Navigator](https://nearshorenavigator.com/en/insights/top-10-shelter-companies-in-mexico-2026-matrix)); (b) the industrial real estate commission (landlord pays the tenant rep broker, typically a percentage of total lease value; CBRE/JLL/Prologis dominate); (c) consultant retainers for site selection / IMMEX setup (Prodensa, Mexecution, Calder & Vale, Nearshore Navigator sell these; prices undisclosed). **There is no published finder-fee market for introducing a US manufacturer to a shelter.** Tetakawi explicitly warns clients to ask advisors whether they take commissions from brokers/developers ([Tetakawi](https://tetakawi.com/blog/a-guide-to-partnering-with-shelter-services-companies-in-mexico/)). In practice, shelters and industrial brokers *do* pay introduction fees privately (commonly a share of first-year fees or 10–25% of the broker's commission), but you must negotiate it case by case and disclose it.

**Real numbers.** A 100-operator shelter client at ~$250/employee/month is ~$300k/yr of shelter revenue; a 10–15% first-year referral is $30–45k. An industrial lease for 100,000 sq ft at $8/sq ft on a 5-year term is $4M of lease value; a 4–6% broker fee is $160–240k, and a 20% referral split is $32–48k. So a *placed* client is worth $30–90k to an introducer. $1M = 12–30 placed clients. Shelter-based launches take 30–60 days after facility occupancy but the *decision* cycle for a US manufacturer is 9–18 months.

**Regulatory risk in 2026:** Mexico's Economy Ministry cancelled/suspended 770+ IMMEX programs in 2025 and suspended 441 more in June 2026 ([TradeFlex](https://trade-flex.com/nearshoring-in-mexico-and-immex-when-a-regulatory-scare-puts-the-opportunity-at-risk/)), and the US declined USMCA renewal — both increase demand for competent guidance and the value of due diligence.

**Legal.** No license to introduce. Mexican real estate brokerage is state-regulated (Nuevo León, Quintana Roo, Jalisco etc. require registration); you should split with a registered Mexican broker rather than act as one. Disclose every fee to the client (conflict of interest is the industry's known problem).

**Capital:** $3–8k (travel to Monterrey/Bajío/Tijuana, a few industrial-park visits).

**What AI automates:** identifying US companies with China exposure (10-K/10-Q tariff disclosures, import records via bill-of-lading data), tariff-delta modeling (Asia at 12.5% + 232 vs USMCA 0%), site comparison matrices, IMMEX/REPSE compliance checklists, Spanish/English deal memos. **Human:** the plant tour, the CFO conversation, the relationship with 3–5 shelters and 2–3 industrial brokers.

**Colombia / Central America:** Colombia is not CAFTA-DR; it trades under the US-Colombia TPA and faces the 10/12.5% Section 301 unless qualifying under the TPA. CAFTA-DR apparel nearshoring is the live corridor (Guatemala, Honduras, El Salvador, DR); the bottleneck is fabric supply, which is a sourcing problem an agent can solve.

**Spanish vs no Spanish:** Decisive. Shelter and broker relationships are bilingual, but factory-level sourcing (play 2.1 reverse) is Spanish.

**Verdict:** Higher fee per deal than plain export agency, same long cycle, few deals. Best used as the *second product* sold to the same US manufacturers you meet as an export agent. P($1M gross in 24 mo) ≈ 2–3% standalone; folded into play 2.1 in the ranking.

### 2.3 Cross-border real estate

**Selling US property to foreign buyers.** Market shrank to $45.3B / 67,100 homes (NAR 2026). Mexican buyers: 9,400 homes, $5.0B (median around $500k) — the natural Spanish-language segment, concentrated in Texas, California, Florida. **Legal line:** referral fees to unlicensed persons are illegal in Florida, Texas and New York; California recognizes a narrow "finder's exception" (introduction only, no negotiating) ([Mashian Law](https://mashianlaw.com/navigating-real-estate-referral-fees/); [Hondros on RESPA](https://www.hondros.com/resources/blog/referral-fee-violate-respa/)). Florida brokers *may* pay referral fees to brokers licensed in a foreign jurisdiction, with disclosure, and 30% withholding applies absent treaty relief ([Justia answer, Oct 2025](https://answers.justia.com/question/2025/10/12/can-a-foreign-real-estate-agent-get-a-re-1079201)). Practical path: **get a US salesperson license** ($500–1,500, 2–4 months) and hang it with a brokerage; then referral fees of 20–35% of gross commission are standard broker-to-broker ([Propphy](https://www.propphy.com/blog/real-estate-referral-fees-guide-2026)). On a $500k home at 2.5% buyer-side, a 25% referral is ~$3,100; a 35% referral ~$4,400. NAR's CIPS designation is the recognized credential.

**Selling foreign property to US buyers.** Mexico: no federal license; Quintana Roo and several states require registration; commissions 5–7%, developers often pay double-sided on pre-construction ([Riviera Maya Insider](https://rivieramayarealestateinsider.com/buying-a-property-in-mexico/)); Mexico Real Estate Group pays referrers 25% of its commission ([MREG referral page](https://www.mexicorealestategroup.com/referral/)) — on a $400k condo at 6% that's ~$6,000 per closing. Compliance risk: 29 Tulum developments flagged as unpermitted and 26 developers shut down in Sept 2025 ([EV Tulum](https://evtulum.com/blog/buyer-alert-29-tulum-developments-flagged-by-government)). Costa Rica: historically unlicensed, referral splits similar. Portugal: mediation requires an AMI license, so you refer to an AMI agency for a split; golden visa real estate was removed in 2023, only the €500k fund route remains ([Immigrant Invest](https://immigrantinvest.com/blog/rp-in-portugal-invest-funds/)). Dominican Republic: unlicensed, developer commissions 5–8% on pre-construction, referral splits negotiable. **No developer "referral program" with published rates turned up for Tulum/Punta Cana; terms are private.**

**Time to first deal:** 2–6 months (buyer-side cycles are shorter than industrial, but cross-border buyers browse for a year).

**Capital:** $1–3k (license, CIPS optional, one familiarization trip).

**What AI automates:** lead capture in Spanish/English, listing translation, fideicomiso/FIRPTA explainers (educational, not legal advice), buyer qualification, nurture sequences, developer due-diligence screens against SEDETUS/permit registries. **Human:** showing property, closing trust, handling a $400k wire.

**Named operators:** Waltz (foreign-buyer mortgage platform, $50M raised, $300M+ in applications, partners with RE/MAX and eXp) ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2025/06/24/waltz-secures-credit-to-scale-foreign-investment-platform/)) — a venture-backed firm, not a solo template. Live and Invest Overseas is a "multimillion-dollar" publisher ([US News bio](https://www.usnews.com/topics/author/kathleen-peddicord)) monetizing conferences and advisories; no disclosed developer-commission revenue.

**Math:** $6k average per closed referral → ~165 closings for $1M. A strong solo agent closes 20–40 a year. **This is a $100–250k business, not a $1M one, on a 24-month horizon.** P($1M gross) ≈ 1–2%.

### 2.4 Import arbitrage

**Honest answer: capital-bound and now structurally worse for small players.** De minimis is gone, so every parcel needs formal/informal entry, and fixed customs costs often exceed the duty on sub-$200 parcels ([Ginger Control](https://gingercontrol.com/blog/de-minimis-repeal-small-importer-impact)). The 2026 tariff stack is 10/12.5% Section 301 + MFN + any 232 (50% metals, 25% derivatives, 25% autos/parts, 25% upholstered furniture). The *mispricing* that exists is **USMCA-qualifying Mexican goods at 0% vs Asian equivalents at 12.5%+**, and the correct way to monetize it without capital is play 2.1/2.2 (represent the Mexican maker), not buying inventory. The one capital-light angle is **IEEPA refund recovery** (see 2.5). First-sale valuation and tariff engineering are real but belong to the importer, not to a $10k arbitrageur. Categories where small importers still win are niche, brand-driven DTC products where margin is 60%+ and duty is a rounding error — that is e-commerce, not trade. P($1M gross with $10k inventory capital) <1%.

### 2.5 Cross-border services arbitrage

**(a) US-priced, AI-delivered services to foreign companies entering the US.** Soft-landing retainers run $1,500/month for 3–6 months (Pro-Latam's published price) and $1,650–3,000/month at co-working-based programs ([Pro-Latam](https://pro-latam.org/services/); [Enrichers](https://www.enrichers.co/soft-landing)); government and university programs (Maryland Global Gateway, Soft Landing NY, SDSU, CIC Miami) are subsidized or free, which caps private pricing. SelectUSA 2026 drew 5,500+ attendees from 100+ markets ([Commerce](https://www.commerce.gov/tags/selectusa-investment-summit)), a concentrated buyer pool. $1M would require ~55 retainers at $1,500 for 12 months — unrealistic in 24 months from zero; $150–300k is realistic. Grant/bid writing for foreign-owned firms is a thin market (most federal grants require US-domiciled entities and foreign ownership complicates SBA programs).

**(b) Tariff-refund and compliance services (the live 2026 angle).** CBP's CAPE refund process for ~$35.5B of IEEPA duties launched 20 April 2026; CBP charges nothing; only the importer of record or a licensed customs broker can file; flat fees of ~$200 first entry + $125/entry for small claims and 4–8% contingency for larger or litigated claims are the market ([FindCustomsBroker](https://findcustomsbroker.com/blog/ieepa-cape-duty-refund-costs-what-importers-need-to-know-in-2026); [CBP](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/trade-remedies/ieepa-duty-refunds)). Entries liquidated more than 180 days ago move to Court of International Trade litigation. Your legal position: you are a *data-prep and advisory* vendor; the IOR self-files in ACE or you partner with a licensed broker who files. At 5% contingency, $1M = $20M of recovered duties — e.g., 10 importers averaging $2M refunds each. Mid-size importers with that profile mostly already have brokers and law firms on it; the under-served tail is importers with $50k–$500k refunds, worth $2.5–25k each to you. Adjacent, durable work: USMCA qualification/certification, HTS reclassification, Section 232 content declarations, and forced-labor (UFLPA) supply-chain documentation, all of which AI drafts well and brokers under-serve. Spanish lets you serve Mexican exporters needing USMCA certificates and US-Hispanic importers.

**(c) Reverse: LatAm/Asian talent + AI for US buyers (staffing / EOR referral).** This is the ranked #1 play; see Section 3 and 4. Documented economics: Deel pays $1,500 per new paying customer ([Deel affiliates](https://www.deel.com/affiliates/)); Remote pays 10–15% revenue share on management fees for 12 months ([Remote](https://remote.com/en-us/partners/affiliates)); Oyster 10–20% ([Oyster](https://oysterhr.com/affiliates)). Staffing-firm placement fees: Somewhere charges a percentage of first-year salary; HireLatam a flat $3,500 ([Virtustant comparison](https://www.virtustant.com/blog/best-nearshore-staffing-companies)). Managed nearshore seats bill $2,500–5,000/month against $1,200–2,500 cost (vendor-claimed 50–88% gross margins; [Virtustant](https://www.virtustant.com/blog/nearshore-staffing-for-agencies)). Mexico's 2021 outsourcing reform (REPSE) and Colombia's labor rules make the **EOR** (Deel/Remote/Oyster/Multiplier or a local PEO) the compliant vehicle; you sell, source, screen and manage; the EOR employs.

### 2.6 Investor-visa and relocation ecosystems

**Caribbean CBI (the only play with government-published agent fees).** Per IMI Daily (15 Jan 2026): St Kitts & Nevis pays **US$50,000 per approved client** under the SISC ($250k minimum) and developers pay $60–70k on public-benefit real estate; St Lucia $35,000 (NEF) / $30,000 (bond); Grenada $30,000 base rising to $35,000 on performance; Antigua $25,000 per NDF/UWI application; Dominica 10% of contribution (~$20,000); Vanuatu $40,000 split between international and local agents; Nauru $25,000. Paid after approval and passport issuance ([IMI Daily](https://www.imidaily.com/analysis/what-governments-pay-cbi-agents-in-commission/)). Applicants must file through a licensed local Authorised Agent; **International Marketing Agents** are authorized by the CIU, must register in St Kitts (since 2023), renew annually, show AML/KYC, and list sub-agents ([CIU St Kitts](https://ciu.gov.kn/international-marketing-agents/); [GoldenVisas](https://www.goldenvisas.com/st-kitts-registered-international-agent)). Realistic path for a newcomer: become a **sub-agent of an existing IMA** for a 30–50% share ($15–25k per file) until you can register.

**Headwinds:** Proclamation 10998 suspends immigrant and B/F/M/J visas for Antigua and Dominica citizens from 1 Jan 2026 (St Kitts, Grenada, St Lucia not named) ([CBI Global](https://citizenshipbyinvestment.global/updates/us-proclamation-10998-antigua-dominica-visa-suspension-2026/)); the EU wants the programs closed by June 2028; ECCIRA now runs shared registers, biometrics and mandatory interviews. The fee per deal is high *because* the product is politically fragile. Your window is 18–24 months.

**EB-5.** Commissions to referral sources are real (~$35,000/investor in *SEC v. Feng* and related 2015 actions; vendor sites cite $40,000+ for a commissioned broker) ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2015-274); [EB5 Visa Investments](https://eb5visainvestments.com/2022/05/25/do-you-need-an-eb-5-broker/)). **Legal constraint is decisive:** anyone in the US receiving transaction-based compensation for EB-5 (a securities offering) must be a FINRA-registered broker-dealer or a registered representative of one ([EB5Investors](https://www.eb5investors.com/magazine/article/shedding-new-light-on-sec-enforcement/); [Canam/Kutak Rock](https://www.canamenterprises.com/2025/12/post-ria-legal-compliance-in-eb5/)). Offshore migration agents have relied on Exchange Act §30(b), but counsel now warns that liability attaches once the investor signs in the US. Compliant path: pass the SIE + Series 82 (private securities offerings) or Series 7 and affiliate with a BD that places EB-5 (several regional centers use one); payouts to registered reps are typically 50–80% of the BD's placement fee. Demand is strong: FY2025 receipts ~6,500, India-led, rural set-aside, with the 30 Sept 2026 grandfathering deadline having pulled filings forward (meaning Q4 2026–2027 demand is *lower* than the 2026 surge). Regional center admin fees $60–80k are on top ([EB5 Visa Investments](https://eb5visainvestments.com/faq/how-negotiable-are-eb-5-regional-center-administration-fees/)).

**E-2.** 51,047 visas issued FY2025 (Japan, Canada, Korea lead; Mexico, Colombia, Argentina, Spain are treaty countries — Brazil is not). Non-attorneys may not give immigration legal advice (UPL; [USCIS](https://www.uscis.gov/scams-fraud-and-misconduct/avoid-scams/become-an-authorized-provider)); what you *can* sell is business-plan writing ($1,650–4,000 flat at Joorney per [VisaPlace](https://www.visaplace.com/?p=54028)), business-acquisition sourcing, and franchise referral (franchisors pay $10–30k broker fees per signed unit, the E-2 franchise channel is well established). A Spanish-speaking E-2 "concierge" bundling plan + franchise/acquisition sourcing + attorney referral is a real $5–20k-per-client business that AI can run 70% of. $1M = 50–200 clients; 24-month realistic: 15–40 clients, $150–400k.

**Portugal golden visa.** Only the €500k fund route (and some cultural/job routes) remain; funds list 2% setup / 1.5–2% management fees and some disclose "0% commission" to distributors ([GetGoldenVisa](https://getgoldenvisa.com/portugal-golden-visa-investment-fund-option)); introducer fees exist (La Vida describes itself as an introducer) but are private and typically 2–5% of subscription paid by the fund manager — **unverified; treat as market hearsay.** Not a Spanish-advantage market.

**Golden Visa referral fees of "$10–50k per applicant" are therefore true only for Caribbean CBI and EB-5; Portugal/Spain are lower and Spain's golden visa ended in April 2025.**

**Spanish vs no Spanish:** Latin America (Mexico, Colombia, Venezuela, Argentina, Ecuador) is a genuine CBI and EB-5/E-2 source market, and it is *under-served by English-only agents*. Without Spanish, you compete in the saturated China/India/Nigeria/Middle East channels where incumbents have offices.

**Verdict:** Highest fee per deal, strongest AI leverage on the paperwork, worst political shelf-life, and it demands HNW access. P($1M gross in 24 mo) ≈ 3–5% with Spanish and a sub-agent deal; ≈1–2% without.

### 2.7 Commodity and surplus brokerage

**Scrap:** the domestic broker's allowance is ~$5/gross ton on ~$400 scrap, ~1%, and Steel Market Update calls the broker's role "diminished" ([SMU](https://www.steelmarketupdate.com/2023/12/15/miller-on-scrap-the-diminished-role-of-the-scrap-broker/)). Export HMS 80/20 was ~$335/mt CFR in early 2025 ([SMU](https://www.steelmarketupdate.com/2025/01/14/export-scrap-market-still-faltering-to-start-2025/)); US ferrous scrap exports fell to ~13M tons in 2025 ([USGS MCS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-iron-steel-scrap.pdf)). To gross $1M at $5/ton you need 200,000 tons — a mid-size yard's annual output. **Ag commodities:** grain/oilseed export is run by ABCD majors; small-agent niches are specialty crops, pulses, pet food, and ingredients at 2–5% commission. **Surplus/liquidation:** real but it's a buy-sell, capital-and-warehouse business. **Scams:** the "LOI → ICPO → FCO → SBLC" sugar/urea/diesel chains are overwhelmingly fraudulent; organized groups target intermediaries with fake tank storage, forged documents, manufactured buyers, and deadline-driven advance fees ([Financely](https://www.financely.io/how-organized-crime-runs-commodity-scams); [Defiant Trading](https://defianttrading.com/how-to-verify-a-supplier/)). An AI team is actually most useful here as a *fraud filter*. P($1M gross) <1%; P(losing your $10k to a fake buyer) materially higher.

### 2.8 International government contracts and development money

USAID is gone as an independent agency (83% of programs cancelled; ops ended 1 July 2025); the surviving State Department foreign-assistance pipeline is smaller and slower. UN system procurement was $25.7B in 2024, with the US the largest supplier country at $2.1B — but that is dominated by pharmaceuticals, logistics and large contractors; UNOPS alone won 51 World Bank contracts worth $396.5M in FY2026 ([Devex](https://www.devex.com/news/who-are-the-world-bank-s-top-contractors-in-2026-112973)). Individual consultant contracts (3–12 months) are the accessible channel, require a supplier profile, and take 4–14 months from notice to award ([BidClarity guide](https://bidclarity.ai/resources/world-bank-sme-guide), vendor source). UN individual contractor rates run ~$190–1,100/day by level ([UN Vacancies](https://unvacancies.org/guides/un-consultant-fee-rates)). The World Bank is reportedly ending STC appointments after July 2026. **For a new entrant with no past performance, 24 months yields at best a couple of sub-$100k consultancies.** The AI team *is* good at monitoring UNGM/dgMarket/IDB notices and drafting EOIs — useful as a side-channel for play 2.5, not a core. P($1M) <1%.

### 2.9 Remittance-adjacent and diaspora businesses

The 1% remittance excise applies only to cash-funded transfers; bank/card-funded transfers are exempt, and providers must collect and report on Form 720 ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/treasury-irs-issue-proposed-regulations-on-the-new-remittance-transfer-tax-established-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill)). The remittance *rails* business is licensed (state MTLs, FinCEN MSB) and capital-heavy — out of scope. What a bilingual operator *can* intermediate at scale: (a) Mexican nationals buying US homes ($5.0B, 9,400 homes; play 2.3); (b) US-Hispanic food brands and "nostalgia" products — 58% of general-population consumers shopped Mexican flavors in 2026 ([Abasto](https://abasto.com/en/advice/hispanic-brands-us-leading-2026/)) — i.e., representing Mexican food makers to US Hispanic retail (play 2.1 reverse); (c) diaspora entrepreneurs' E-2 and relocation needs (play 2.6); (d) LatAm talent for US employers (play 2.5c). The diaspora is a **channel**, not a business. No standalone remittance play clears the bar.

## 4. Defending the top 2 with a concrete first 60 days

### 4.1 Play 1 — LatAm talent + AI managed services for US SMBs

**Thesis.** US agencies, accounting/bookkeeping firms, law firms, medical practices, property managers and SaaS companies pay $60–130k for roles that a vetted Colombian/Mexican/Argentine professional plus an AI stack delivers at $35–55k ([CrossBridge](https://www.gocrossbridge.com/blog/the-roi-of-nearshore-staffing-a-breakdown-for-finance-minded-founders), vendor source). You sell the *outcome* (a bilingual AR clerk who closes books with AI, an SDR running your outbound, a paralegal doing intake), not a résumé. The EOR (Deel/Remote/Oyster) makes it compliant and shifts payroll timing. Spanish lets you recruit and manage the talent directly; without Spanish you can still run it in Colombia/Argentina's English-fluent professional pool, with the AI team doing bilingual screening — call it a 30% handicap, not a blocker.

**Why it's #1.** (a) No license. (b) First dollar in 30–60 days. (c) The AI team *is* the product differentiator — every seat ships with an AI workflow you build. (d) Gross revenue scales with seats, so $1M gross = ~30 seats; a good solo operator with AI can plausibly manage 30 seats. (e) Three documented partner programs pay you on top (Deel $1,500/customer; Remote 10–15%; Oyster 10–20%).

**Risks.** Margin compression (hundreds of nearshore shops); misclassification if you skip the EOR; cash-flow timing; client churn at 6–12 months; your talent getting hired away (use 6-month guarantees and conversion fees).

**First 60 days.**
- *Days 1–7:* Pick two verticals where the Owner has any network (e.g., US accounting firms and marketing agencies). AI agents build a 1,000-company ICP list each (firm size 5–50, hiring signals, job posts for bookkeeper/SDR/paralegal). Form the LLC, open the EOR partner accounts (Deel, Remote, Oyster — apply to all three affiliate/partner programs), set up ATS/CRM.
- *Days 8–21:* AI recruiting agents source and pre-screen 200 candidates in Colombia/Mexico/Argentina (LinkedIn, Computrabajo, Torre, Bumeran); human-interview the top 30; build a bench of 10 "ready" profiles with AI-workflow portfolios (e.g., a bookkeeper who demos month-end close with an AI assistant). Draft the MSA/SOW with a 50% first-month deposit and 6-month replacement guarantee.
- *Days 8–30:* Outbound: 100 personalized emails/day per vertical plus LinkedIn; AI writes, the Owner takes every discovery call (target 15 calls/week). Offer a 2-week paid pilot at $1,500.
- *Days 31–45:* Close 3–5 pilots. Onboard talent through the EOR. Build the per-seat AI playbook (SOPs, tools, QA).
- *Days 46–60:* Convert pilots to $3,000–4,500/month seats; ask each for two referrals; publish two Spanish/English case studies. Target at day 60: 5–8 seats, ~$20–30k MRR, path to 30 seats by month 12.
- *Kill criteria:* fewer than 3 paid pilots by day 45 → change vertical; CAC above one month's margin → stop ads, go referral-only.

### 4.2 Play 2 — Investor-migration intermediary (Spanish-market CBI + E-2 concierge, EB-5 only via a broker-dealer)

**Thesis.** Latin American HNW families (Mexico, Colombia, Venezuela, Ecuador, Argentina) buy second citizenships and US investor visas, and the English-language agent industry under-serves them. Government-published fees are $25–50k per approved CBI file; E-2 concierge packages are $5–20k; EB-5 adds $15–40k per investor once you're a registered rep. The AI team does what a $150k/year case-management team does: document checklists, source-of-funds narratives (prepared for the licensed agent/attorney, not legal advice), business plans, translations, KYC prep, nurture content.

**Why it's #2 and not #1.** Access. The person who makes $1M here closes 25–40 HNW files in two years, which means being inside wealth-manager, private-banker and family-office referral circles. Fee-per-deal is the best on this list; deal flow is the worst. And the product is politically perishable (EU 2028, Proclamation 10998).

**Legal rails.** (1) CBI: sign as a sub-agent of a CIU-authorized International Marketing Agent for St Kitts, St Lucia and Grenada (avoid Antigua/Dominica while 10998 is in force); register as IMA in St Kitts once you have 5+ files. (2) US immigration: no legal advice; every client is co-engaged with a licensed immigration attorney; you sell business plans, business/franchise sourcing, relocation logistics. (3) EB-5: do not touch a dollar of transaction-based compensation until you hold SIE + Series 82 and a BD affiliation; until then, refer to the attorney with zero fee. (4) Disclose every commission in writing to the client.

**First 60 days.**
- *Days 1–14:* AI agents compile the licensed-agent universe (CIU lists for St Kitts/St Lucia/Grenada; IMI's approved/blacklisted lists), identify 20 IMAs that want Spanish-market sub-agents, and 15 immigration attorneys (Miami, Houston, Mexico City) who do E-2/EB-5 for Latin Americans. Owner books 10 calls, signs one IMA sub-agent agreement (target 40–50% share) and two attorney referral MOUs. Register for the SIE exam.
- *Days 15–30:* Build the Spanish-language authority asset: a 30-page "Segundo pasaporte y visa de inversionista en EE.UU. 2026" guide (AI-drafted, attorney-reviewed), a webinar, and a LinkedIn/YouTube presence. AI runs outreach to 300 Latin American wealth advisors, private bankers, family-office staff and chambers (COMCE, AmCham Mexico/Colombia, Venezuelan diaspora business groups in Miami/Houston/Madrid).
- *Days 31–45:* Run two webinars (Spanish), one in-person dinner in Miami or Houston with the IMA and the attorney. Target 40 qualified conversations, 8 formal consultations, 2 signed E-2 concierge engagements ($7,500 each, 50% upfront) and 1–2 CBI files in document collection.
- *Days 46–60:* File the first CBI application through the IMA; deliver the first E-2 business plan; systematize the case-management playbook in the AI team. Take the SIE. Day-60 scorecard: $10–20k cash collected (E-2), $50–100k of CBI fees in pipeline (paid on approval, 4–8 months out).
- *Kill criteria:* fewer than 20 qualified HNW conversations by day 45 means the network isn't there; fold the E-2 concierge product into Play 1's client base (US SMB owners with foreign partners) and drop CBI.

## 5. Odds and what would change them

- **The honest base rate** for a solo, no-capital operator reaching $1M gross in any of these within 24 months is low single digits. The AI team changes the *throughput* of research, outreach and paperwork — roughly 3–5× a solo human — but it does not change the two things that gate every cross-border intermediary business: counterparty trust and deal cycle time.
- **Spanish moves the odds** in plays 1, 2, 3 and 5 by roughly 2× because Mexico/Central America/Colombia are where the 2026 trade and migration flows are, and because the English-only competition is thick everywhere else.
- **What would push play 1 above 20%:** one anchor client (an agency or accounting firm with 10+ seats) in the first 90 days, or a partnership with a US firm that already sells to the ICP.
- **What would push play 2 above 10%:** an existing relationship with a Latin American private bank or multi-family office, or a lawyer partner who hands over their overflow.
- **What kills the whole plan:** treating gross as net (staffing), paying advance fees to anyone (commodities), taking undisclosed commissions (real estate, nearshoring, EB-5), or giving immigration/tax advice without a license.
