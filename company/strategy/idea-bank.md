## The ten, one paragraph each

**1. Grant & Bid Studio.** 588,000 US nonprofits have budgets under $1M and no grant writer.
40% of state and local government solicitations draw fewer than three bids. 83% of one federal
resilience program's awards went to high-capacity counties, meaning 16,000 small towns rarely
apply. Freelance grant writers charge $1,500-5,000 per proposal or $2,000-6,000 a month. We
charge $1,250 a month or $1,800 a proposal, found inbound through Upwork, a free "grant readiness
score," and bookkeeper referrals. The grant-rfp-writer skill, Drive, Calendar and DocuSign already
run most of it. The Owner reviews drafts and talks to executive directors. Ethics rule: no
contingency fees on grants.

**2. Get-Paid.** Intuit's 2025 data: 56% of US small businesses are owed money, averaging
$17,500. Collection agencies take 25-50%. Chaser-style software costs $259 a month and the owner
still has to run it. We connect to the client's QuickBooks, pull the aging report, run polite
multi-channel follow-up sequences with payment links, and charge $199 a month plus 8% of what we
recover past 60 days. Performance pricing sells itself. Bookkeepers refer us because they hate
chasing. The QuickBooks connector and invoice-chase skill already exist. Needs a text-message send
path and carrier registration.

**3. Denial Desk.** 80.7% of appealed Medicare Advantage prior-auth denials were overturned in
2024, but only 11.5% were appealed. Physicians handle about 40 prior auths a week. A practice
collecting $3M a year is worth roughly $34k a year to us at 20-35% of recoveries. Forty practices
is a seven-figure business. No license needed, only a HIPAA agreement. The catch: outbound sales
to practice managers and a cash lag on contingency. Strong year-two product once we have
bookkeeping relationships.

**4. Back Office to Buyout.** Bookkeeping and tax practices sell at about 1.1x revenue and 2.3x
earnings, and retiring owners routinely carry 30-50% of the price as a seller note. 300,000
accountants have left the field and CPA candidates are down 43%. Start with an AI-delivered
monthly finance pulse at $750-1,500 a month (between QuickBooks Live and a fractional CFO), bank
the profit, and buy a $150-300k practice in year two with seller financing and an SBA loan. Then
add two more. This is the only idea on the list where the evidence on prices is 5 out of 5 and
the end state is a sellable asset. Slowest to start, best path to $1M.

**5. Home-Turf Tool.** The strongest predictor of a high-growth founder in the academic
literature is prior experience in the industry. Whatever industry the Owner knows from the
inside, there is a workflow that a $5-15k AI install plus a retainer fixes, sold warm to former
employers and peers, no cold calls. Convert to software once five clients share the workflow.
Cannot be scored properly until `boss-profile.md` is filled in.

**6. Compliance Desk.** Every startup needs SOC 2 before its first enterprise deal. Readiness
projects run $4-17k, fractional compliance retainers $3-12k a month, and the Vanta and Drata
partner directories send inbound leads. About 80% agent-operable. Buyers are technical and
price-aware, so margins compress faster than in the other ideas.

**7. Micro-Acquisition Flywheel.** Sub-$100k software businesses on Acquire.com sell at a median
3.7x net income and sit 90 days on the market. Buy two or three neglected $5-25k tools or browser
extensions, fix the bugs and marketing with agents, resell. Zero cold selling, pure building. The
honest problem: strong data on prices, almost no data on what happens after people buy. Treat it
as a training ground for diligence and agent operations, and as the Owner's "make things" lane.

**8. Fractional AI-Native COO.** Operations leadership retainers cluster at $5-15k a month. Three
clients is $15-30k a month with agents doing the work. It is a job, not equity, and it needs
warm introductions. Its role is to fund ideas 4 or 7, not to be the destination.

**9. Never Miss a Lead.** Thesis v1. Still real, still scored, but it carries the highest
cold-selling burden on the list and competes with $49-299 apps. Kept as a product line we can
add later for home-service clients we meet through bookkeepers.

**10. The Lab.** SBIR was reauthorized in April 2026 through 2031 with a solicitation backlog.
Phase I grants are $150-300k for genuine R&D with no cold selling and no dilution. Odds are low
(about 5%) and only a real research angle qualifies. But the Owner wants to do science and make
things, so the company gets a research lane with capped hours, run by a new Lab agent. Its first
jobs: find three SBIR topics that fit, and run small experiments that improve our own products.

## What the six lenses agreed on

- Fast wealth from zero comes from owning equity in a skill-intensive B2B business, incorporated,
  with the owner working hard. That is the only profile the peer-reviewed literature backs.
- The open arbitrage in 2026 is in work that is text-heavy, deadline-driven, sits inside a
  liability-bearing workflow, and is bought by time-starved owners rather than price-sophisticated
  ones: money owed, money available, money denied.
- Buying a business beats starting one for reaching seven figures of equity, but requires $75-150k
  first. So the cash business exists to fund the acquisition.
- Ideas 1, 2, 3 and 4 share one buyer: owner-operated organizations with QuickBooks, a bookkeeper,
  and no CFO. They share one channel: bookkeepers and CPAs who refer. That is a company, not a list.
