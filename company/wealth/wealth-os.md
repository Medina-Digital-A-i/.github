# Wealth Operating System

_Owner: CFO. Educational only. A licensed CPA and attorney confirm every election before it is
made. Figures are 2026 and come from `company/research/research-wealth-mechanics.md` (70+
sources, Fed Survey of Consumer Finances 2025, IRS notices, Ramsey study, Stanley)._

## The three facts that shape everything

1. **The first million is earned and saved, not traded.** Ramsey's 10,000-millionaire study:
   79% inherited nothing, a third never earned six figures in a year, median path 28 years,
   crossing $1M at 49. Only 5% did it in under 10 years. Those 5% had one thing in common: a
   much higher income, usually from owning a business.
2. **Business ownership is the differentiator.** 40% of top-decile families own a private
   business, versus 8% in the bottom half. Most businesses are jobs. A few are wealth engines.
   The difference is whether the business can run and be sold without the owner.
3. **Contribution rate beats return rate for the first million.** From zero at 7% real returns:

| Saved per month | Years to $1M |
|-----------------|--------------|
| $1,000 | 28 |
| $2,000 | 20 |
| $3,000 | 15.5 |
| $5,000 | 11 |
| $8,000 | 8 |
| $10,000 | 6.7 |

   Going from $1k to $5k a month cuts 17 years. Going from 7% to 10% returns cuts 6. The lever
   is income. That is why the company exists.

## The "secrets," which are really just structure

| Tool | $0-100k income | $100k-500k | $1M+ |
|------|----------------|------------|------|
| LLC for liability | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| S-corp election | Rarely (over ~$70k profit) | **Core** | Yes, unless C-corp for QSBS |
| QBI deduction (now permanent) | Automatic | **Core, plan around $201,750 single / $403,500 joint** | Mostly phased out for service businesses |
| Solo 401(k) | Yes, Roth deferrals | **Max $72,000** ($24,500 deferral + employer) | Max + mega backdoor + cash balance plan |
| HSA ($4,400 / $8,750) | **Yes** | Yes | Yes |
| Roth IRA (phase-out $153k-168k single) | Direct | Backdoor | Mega backdoor, conversions |
| Home office | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Section 179 ($2.56M) / 100% bonus depreciation | Expense equipment | Equipment, vehicles if real need | Cost segregation |
| Augusta rule (rent home to S-corp 14 days) | No | Yes | Yes |
| Hire kids or spouse | Maybe | Yes | Yes |
| QSBS (50/75/100% exclusion at 3/4/5 years, $15M cap) | Plan entity early | Decide C vs S | **Core on exit** |
| Real estate depreciation, 1031 | No | Side allocation | Yes |
| Buy, borrow, die | No | No | Yes |
| Donor-advised fund (0.5% of AGI floor) | Cash gifts | Bunching | CRTs, foundations |

## The order of operations

### Month 1 (a few hundred dollars, ~10 hours)
- [ ] Business checking account and business card. Never commingle.
- [ ] LLC in home state, or sole prop for the first months if cash is tight. EIN is free.
- [ ] Bookkeeping from day one. Spreadsheet is fine. Categorize monthly.
- [ ] Roth IRA opened, automatic monthly transfer, even $100.
- [ ] Emergency fund started in high-yield savings. Target 1 month of expenses first.
- [ ] Health insurance. Bronze/catastrophic ACA plans are HSA-eligible in 2026. Open the HSA.
- [ ] Tax reserve: 25-35% of every payment into a separate savings account. Quarterly estimates
      due Apr 15, Jun 15, Sep 15, Jan 15.

### Month 3
- [ ] Review entity against real profit run-rate. S-corp talk with CPA at ~$70-80k profit.
- [ ] Solo 401(k) at a low-cost brokerage. Roth deferrals while the bracket is low.
- [ ] Home office claimed and documented.
- [ ] **Automate the waterfall** on the day money lands: tax reserve (30%) → emergency fund (to
      6 months) → Roth IRA → Solo 401(k) → taxable brokerage.
- [ ] **Written investment policy**: total-market or global index fund in every account,
      rebalanced annually. No individual stocks, no crypto beyond what you can lose, no trading.
      Revisit once a year, not when the market moves.
- [ ] Term life and disability insurance if anyone depends on the income.

### Year 1
- [ ] CPA for the first business return plus a one-hour planning meeting: S-corp timing, QBI,
      Solo 401(k) funding, self-employed health insurance deduction.
- [ ] Emergency fund to 6 months personal plus 1-3 months business fixed costs.
- [ ] Decide the long-term entity path. Cash-flow business: S-corp. Software you may sell:
      C-corp early to start the QSBS clock.
- [ ] Annual review: net worth statement, savings rate, tax reserve, insurance, beneficiaries.

### At $5k/month revenue
S-corp if profit is consistently ~$70k+. Max HSA. $1-2k/month into Roth and Solo 401(k). Raise
prices or drop the bottom 20% of clients. Confirm QBI is claimed.

### At $10k/month revenue
S-corp almost certainly correct; salary set with CPA. Max Solo 401(k). Backdoor Roth over the
phase-out. Augusta rule for real planning days. Hire family if the work is real. Taxable
brokerage with asset location. Savings target **30-40% of gross profit**. At $5k/month invested,
about 11 years to $1M.

### At $25k/month revenue
Inside the QBI phase-in; CPA models retirement contributions every November to pull taxable
income back under. Mega backdoor Roth. Cash balance plan if 40+. Product company: finalize C-corp
and QSBS. Service company: build a transferable asset or accept it is a high-income job and run a
30%+ savings rate. **Diversify ruthlessly**: business reinvestment only to what the business
needs; everything else to the boring portfolio. Will, trust, beneficiaries, continuity plan.
At a 40% savings rate (~$120k/yr), about **7 years to $1M**. That is the "10 years, not 28" path,
and it runs entirely through income.

## Why we do not trade our way there

79% of active large-cap funds trailed the S&P 500 in 2025 and about 90% over 15 years. 97% of
persistent day traders lost money. 73-81% of retail bitcoin buyers likely lost. Investors forfeit
about 1.2 points a year to timing. The boring portfolio wins because it does nothing.

## Billionaire lessons that actually transfer

- **Own equity in something that scales** (Bezos, Musk, Thiel). For us: the software rung.
- **Diversify once you have won** (Cuban sold, hedged, and never looked back).
- **Compounding needs time and no interruptions** (Buffett). No leverage, no panic selling.
- **Write the principles down and follow them when it hurts** (Dalio). That is this file.
- What does not transfer: timing, capital access, and the hundred people with the same plan who
  are not in the biography.
